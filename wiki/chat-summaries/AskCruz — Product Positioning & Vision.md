# AskCruz — Product Positioning & Vision

_One-sentence description of what this cluster of conversations covers._

Seven Raj conversations (Aug 2–4, 2026) working out what AskCruz/GFC actually is, how it's positioned against competitors like Palantir, what its "Company Brain" needs to contain, its Sabre Alloys go-to-market meeting, its access-rights architecture, and the personal-stakes tradeoff between a $50M and $1B five-year outcome.

## Summary

AskCruz is the flagship product of Ganesh Fruit Company (GFC), Raj's separate holding company (named after his grandfather's fruit business) built alongside EOXS. The product went through two prior names — Podar, then Krinox — before permanently settling on AskCruz (askcruz.com) as of the Aug 3 access-rights thread, where Raj explicitly instructed Claude to stop using "Krinox" entirely. "Cruz" is the AI agent persona embodied inside the product.

Positioning, worked out across the Aug 4 overview thread and the Aug 2 Palantir-comparison thread: AskCruz is explicitly an "AI operating system for businesses," not a services/consulting company — a Claude account wired into an Obsidian Vault ("OV") that continuously ingests live ERP data, real-time email, call transcripts, and a static knowledge-base layer. It is a superset of IRIS (the live-ERP + Claude natural-language BI query layer), whose IP is being formally acquired from EOXS for $10,000 into GFC. Zola (a live-call AI assistant, EOXS-only R&D, demoed to Sabre Alloys) is explicitly not part of AskCruz.

The product vision is staged in layers: Company Brain → Company Hands → Digital Workforce → Generic Intelligence → Professional/Human DNA → Company Personalization → Marketplace → Continuous Learning. "Human DNA" is the same lineage as EOXS's "AI Joe" Gerdau-veteran-cloning concept. One concrete spinoff concept, Agent Vinod, would ingest a client org's daily Claude conversation threads and build a searchable knowledge base so a CEO can ask what their team has been working on or struggling with.

Versus Palantir (Aug 2): same underlying model — a unified layer over messy siloed data, queried via AI/analyst interface, requiring heavy bespoke per-client integration ("forward-deployed engineering" at Palantir, the same animal as AskCruz's flagged "30 client integrations in 90 days" delivery problem). Real differences: Palantir built its ontology/integration engine (Foundry/Gotham) over a decade before layering AI on top (AIP), while AskCruz is AI-first with a comparatively lightweight OV leaning on Claude as the reasoning layer. Palantir's $1M+ contracts can absorb months of bespoke engineer time per client; AskCruz's $30K ACV cannot without an order-of-magnitude drop in delivery cost per client — this is flagged as the real constraint, not company size. Palantir's counterpart is usually a client IT/data team; a 30–200 employee AskCruz target company mostly has none, so AskCruz must do more of its own plumbing. If AskCruz's delivery model ends up looking like Palantir's forward-deployed teams, that directly contradicts the GFC charter's "not a services business" framing.

ICP and GTM (Aug 4): mid-market CEO or P&L owner who can approve a $30K deal solo. No SEO — outbound sales plus social content, leveraging Raj's 20K LinkedIn connections and six company profiles. Funnel CTA deliberately changed from "waitlist" to "book a fit call" (sales-led, not product-led). Revenue target: ~30 clients at $30K ACV within three months of launch — the single highest-risk gap in the plan, since positioning/ICP/pricing/GTM all have defined answers but hitting ~30 bespoke integrations in 90 days does not.

Equity/roles (corrected 2026-08-02, per the gfc-askcruz-1b-vs-50m-vision thread): Raj holds 100% equity in AskCruz/GFC — this supersedes an earlier 2026-07-23 Founder Charter that had specified a 51/20/20/9 split. Ron and Ayan hold no equity, contributing role/time instead: Raj is CEO (vision, sales, marketing, fundraising, capital allocation, final authority); Ron handles product strategy, customer implementation/onboarding/support, delivery (50% Year One, full-time after); Ayan handles engineering/AI architecture/platform scalability full-time from day one. No founder salary entitlement; all IP created belongs to the company. Raj's initial contribution: $50,000 cash plus the acquired IRIS IP.

The $50M vs $1B five-year path comparison (Aug 2): Path A ($50M) is an operator-CEO grind, 55–65 hrs/week early dropping to 45–55, occasional travel, maybe one modest raise, real weekends back by Year 3, worst case still leaves a $2–5M ARR profitable business Raj fully owns. Path B ($1B) is a fundraiser-evangelist-CEO job, 75–90 hrs/week early staying at 65–80 indefinitely, heavy travel, 2–3 raises every 12–18 months, forces resolving Ron/Ayan equity immediately, effectively no time off until exit, and risks diluting Raj to 15–30% ownership by year 5 with no consolation-prize business if it fails, since Path B explicitly abandons the steel-network moat that makes Path A safe.

Sabre Alloys pitch prep (Aug 2–3): Michael Mercadante (CEO/Owner) is a strategic sponsor who independently built a 45-page self-trained ChatGPT sales/pricing playbook and compiled a 20-category AI requirements spec in June 2026 — the most detailed product-engagement document any EOXS client has produced. He wants a daily plain-English management briefing (cash risk, late orders, AR, margin exceptions, inventory, vendor problems), source-traceable. He issued a churn threat in Oct 2024. Tye Webb (COO/VP Operations) is the highest-volume Sabre contact (477 threads), blunt, low tolerance for disruption, independently proposed the same "AI sales director" concept EOXS is building, and is already live-testing the Claude account against real RFQs — but is currently managing an unresolved, actively-escalated complaint, and the relationship is flagged "at risk" in the vault. A discrepancy was flagged: Raj described 23 months of free IRIS usage, but the vault shows Sabre's been an EOXS ERP client ~26 months while the actual Claude/IRIS access was only issued ~2 months earlier (May 26, 2026). A 6-slide pitch deck was built and delivered (Sabre_Alloys_EOXS_Meeting.pptx) following a 30-minute structure: acknowledge open items first, recap what's live, show what's next (Sales Director agent + Zola), make the ask (formalize the free pilot into a structured plan, numbers kept directional since per-seat pricing isn't finalized), close with one concrete next step.

Access rights (Aug 3): EOXS will be AskCruz's first customer, with Sabre Alloys as the second pilot customer in the steel industry (Raj said "Shabbor," read by Claude as Sabre — flagged as unconfirmed). Raj explicitly rejected static ERP-style role-based access control in favor of an "intelligent," context/purpose-based model where what a user is asking Cruz determines what data they can see, rather than a fixed role→permission mapping. The open architectural question flagged: the tenant boundary between an EOXS-internal Cruz instance and Sabre's own Cruz instance — what happens if one can see the other's data.

Company Brain requirements (Aug 3, "50ft deep" pass): categorized as must-have vs. would-like, grounded in IRIS's real deployment history and known failure modes (detailed in Tribal Knowledge below).

Positioning council request (Aug 3): Raj asked Claude to review competitor websites and convene a "council" of domain experts (per the sales-strategist skill's Council mode — 5 expert seats plus Claude as facilitator) to help build the AskCruz landing-page positioning. The thread stalled waiting on Raj to supply actual competitor URLs (none were sent) — no positioning conclusions were reached in this thread.

Logo/naming discussion (Aug 4): keep "AskCruz" as the registered brand (domain, equity docs, GTM material already anchor there) — the "Ask" prefix does real explanatory work in year one since the ICP doesn't know what Cruz is yet (compared to Ask Jeeves/Ask.com). Recommended a wordmark where "Cruz" carries the dominant visual weight with "Ask" as a smaller, droppable lockup element (e.g., dropped in a favicon). Recommended avoiding an avatar/face icon on the corporate mark, since that would drift toward "chatbot mascot" and undercut the "operating system" positioning being sold to a mid-market CEO writing a $30K check.
