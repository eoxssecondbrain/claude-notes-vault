User: What is the difference between stick foundation, cream-to-powder, and BB/CC cream formulations in the wiki? Which is best for oily skin in humidity?

Assistant: This falls outside the eoxs-data-general and sales-strategist skill scope — it's the SUGAR Cosmetics complexion-bases wiki, not EOXS data. Flagging that per your instruction, then proceeding since it's a straightforward wiki lookup, not a research/strategy task.

Formulation | Format | Finish | Core chemistry | Best for
---|---|---|---|---
Stick foundation | Portable stick | Natural matte | High pigment-to-binder ratio with sub-10-micron film thickness — full-to-sheer coverage without caking or "heat slide" | All skin types; quick application, spot coverage, on-the-go touch-ups
Cream-to-powder | Compact cream | Velvet matte | Phase inversion — volatile silicones in the emulsion evaporate on skin contact, triggering silica spherical beads to form a dry, porous matrix that continuously traps sebum | Oily skin, high humidity, monsoon conditions
BB/CC cream | Lightweight cream | Sheer, natural (BB = blemish-balm coverage + skincare actives; CC = color-correcting, tone-evening) | Wiki only holds blog citations for this category — no extracted chemistry breakdown, so this is the thinnest entry of the three | Quick daily coverage, minimal-makeup days

Direct answer: cream-to-powder is the better formulation for oily skin in humidity. The phase-inversion mechanism is built to keep working through the day — sebum keeps getting absorbed into the porous matrix rather than sitting on top of a film that breaks down as oil production ramps up in heat, which is what happens to stick and BB/CC formulas over several hours. The wiki's own formula-selection guide pairs Mettle Cream To Powder Foundation with a translucent powder specifically for "oily T-zone in monsoon."

Caveat: the BB/CC leg of this comparison is weak. That entry in the vault is a source citation pointing to a SUGAR blog post, not a synthesized chemistry note like the other two. If you want a real formulation-level comparison — silicone vs. water base, SPF load, pigment percentage — that's not in the wiki yet; getting it would mean pulling from the blog copy itself rather than a vetted internal note.
