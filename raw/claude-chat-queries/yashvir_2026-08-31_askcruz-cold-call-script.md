User: like prepare a common scrpt that can be used for everyone, make sure the script is easy to understand by everyone and how we can explain what askcruz is and how it can be benaficial in the simplest language possible.

Assistant: Universal script — works regardless of who picks up, in plain language

Cold opener

"Hi [Name], it's Yash — I'll be straight with you, this is a cold call. I've got one quick question about how your team finds answers when information's spread across different systems. Got 20 seconds?"
[pause]

If "go ahead": "I'm honestly not sure if you're the right person for this, but I had a quick question about how [Company] handles that day to day."

Gatekeeper path

"Hi, I'm trying to reach whoever would know how [Company]'s team finds things out — like what's in stock, what was promised to a customer, or why a number changed — when that info's spread across different systems. Who would that be?"

Reason for the call, plain language, no industry jargon

"Most companies I talk to run into the same thing — someone needs an answer fast, and it's scattered across a few different systems, or it's stuck in one person's head who might not even be around right now. I wanted to ask if that ever slows your team down."

Discovery, simple version

Situation: "When someone needs to find something out fast, is that usually quick, or does it take some digging around?"
Problem: "How often does that actually slow things down, or lead to a mistake?"
Implication: "What does that end up costing you — time, a frustrated customer, a decision made on a guess instead of the real answer?"
Need-payoff: "If anyone on your team could just ask a plain question and get the right answer right away, what would that be worth to you?"

What AskCruz is, in the simplest possible terms

"So here's what we actually do. AskCruz is like having a smart assistant that already knows your company. Instead of digging through emails, spreadsheets, or different systems, or tracking someone down to ask, you just ask a plain question — like 'what did we promise this customer' or 'what's actually in stock' — and it gives you the answer right away. It doesn't replace the systems you already use. It just connects to them and makes the answer easy to get to, for anyone on your team who needs it."

The benefit, in one line

"Short version — it saves your team the time they spend hunting for answers, and it cuts down on the mistakes that happen when someone guesses instead of waiting to find out for sure."

Close, works for any role

"Would you be the right person to look at something like that, or is there someone else on your team I should talk to?"
"Is this worth 20 minutes to actually see how it'd work for you?"

Voicemail

"Hi [Name], it's Yash — calling about how your team finds answers when information's scattered across systems. Not a general check-in, just a quick question. My number's [number] if you want to skip the back-and-forth."

This version deliberately drops the role-specific pain menus and the MEDDIC/BANT-flavored phrasing from the earlier scripts — it's meant as your default when you don't know who's answering, or as a simple explanation you can drop into any conversation, not a replacement for the sharper role-specific scripts when you do know who you're calling.
