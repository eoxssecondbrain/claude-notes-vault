Script 1 — Quote-to-fulfillment friction

Cold opener: "Hi [Name], it's Yash — I'll be straight with you, this is a cold call. I've got one specific question about how much rework your team does when a quote didn't match what was actually available. You got 20 seconds?"
[pause]
If "go ahead": "I've been looking at how [Company]'s operations team handles orders that come in with mismatched pricing or availability, and I had one specific question. Got 30 seconds?"

Gatekeeper: "Hi, I'm trying to reach whoever runs operations at [Company] — is that you, or is there a Director of Operations or Operations Manager I should be talking to?"
If pushed: "Sure — I had a specific question about how your team handles orders where the quote didn't match what was actually available, and wanted their take directly."

Reason for call: "Most operations leaders I talk to at steel distributors deal with the same thing — a quote goes out based on pricing or inventory that turns out to be off, and operations ends up doing the cleanup. I wanted to ask how that shows up for your team, if at all."

Discovery, Situation: "Walk me through what happens when an order comes in that doesn't match what's actually available or priced correctly — how does your team catch that, and when?"
Discovery, Problem: "How often does your team have to rework or clarify an order because the quote wasn't accurate to begin with?"
Discovery, Implication: "When that happens, what does it cost you — staff time, a delayed order, a customer escalation that lands on your desk instead of sales'? Is that a daily thing, or occasional?"
Discovery, Need-payoff: "If quotes were accurate to actual inventory and pricing before they ever reached your team, what would that free up? Who else deals with the fallout when it goes wrong — your floor staff, customer service?"

Insight: "What I've noticed with operations leaders at distributors like yours — the errors rarely start on the floor, they start upstream when a quote goes out disconnected from what's actually available, and operations is the one who inherits the fix."
