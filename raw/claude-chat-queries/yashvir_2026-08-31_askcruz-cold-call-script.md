Discovery, with examples baked in

Situation: "When someone needs to find something out fast — like what's actually in stock, or what a customer was already promised — is that usually a quick lookup, or does it take some digging across a few systems or asking around?"

Problem: "How often does that kind of digging — checking two or three places, or tracking someone down — actually slow things down or lead to a mistake, like a wrong quote or a promise that doesn't hold up?"

Implication: "What does that end up costing you — say, an order that ships late, a customer told two different things by two different people, or a decision made on a guess because nobody wanted to wait for the real answer?"

Need-payoff: "If anyone on your team could just ask something like 'what's in stock' or 'what did we tell this customer' and get the answer right away instead of digging, what would that be worth to you?"
