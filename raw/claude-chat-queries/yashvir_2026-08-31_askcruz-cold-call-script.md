Assistant: AskCruz cold-calling script, standard version — target: Head of Sales / Director of Sales

Cold opener, net-new account, first touch

"Hi [Name], it's Yash — I'll be straight with you, this is a cold call. I've got one specific question about [pain area]. You got 20 seconds?"
[pause]

If they say "go ahead" or "what's this about":
"I've been looking at how [Company]'s sales team handles [pain area], and I had one specific question. Got 30 seconds?"

Gatekeeper path

"Hi, I'm trying to reach whoever runs sales at [Company] — is that you, or is there a Head of Sales or Sales Director I should be talking to?"

If pushed for a reason:
"Sure — I had a specific question about how your sales team handles [pain area], and wanted their take directly, since it's not really a call I can do justice to secondhand."

Reason for the call, pick one based on pre-call research

"The reason I'm calling — most sales leaders I talk to at steel distributors are dealing with one of these:
— quotes taking longer than they should because pricing and inventory data live in different systems
— reps promising something to a customer that inventory can't actually back up
— no clean record of what was actually promised to a customer, especially when a rep is out or turns over
— details from sales calls not making it into the account record, so the next call starts blind
— new reps taking months to get productive because the real knowledge lives in a couple of veteran heads
I wanted to ask how that shows up for your team, if at all."

Discovery — Situation, common opener regardless of branch

"Walk me through how your team handles [quoting / inventory checks / customer commitments / call follow-up / new-rep ramp-up — match to the branch above] today. Is that info sitting in one place, or scattered across a few systems and people's heads?"

Discovery — Problem, branch to the pain named above

Quoting delays: "When a rep needs to get a fast quote out, how long does that usually take end to end?"
Inventory blind spots: "How often does a rep promise something a customer that inventory can't actually back up?"
Lost commitments: "When a rep is out or leaves, how does anyone else find out what they'd already promised a customer?"
Call follow-up gaps: "After a sales call, how much of what was discussed actually makes it into the account record versus staying in that rep's head?"
New-rep ramp time: "How long does it typically take a new rep to stop needing to interrupt someone senior for routine account questions?"

Discovery — Implication, cost in their own words

"When that happens, what does it actually cost your team — a slower quote cycle, an annoyed customer, a deal that slips because the follow-up dropped?"
"Is that a once-a-month thing, or closer to daily, across the team?"

Discovery — Need-payoff

"If that friction just disappeared — reps could just ask and get the answer instead of hunting or guessing — what would that be worth to your numbers this quarter?"
"Besides you, who else on your team feels this the most — your senior reps, your newer reps, whoever's fielding the escalations?"

Insight — Challenger teach moment, tailor live to their actual answer

"Here's what I've noticed talking to sales leaders at distributors like yours — the real cost usually isn't the time a rep spends searching, it's the deal that slips or the promise that gets broken because the answer lived in someone's head instead of somewhere the whole team could reach. That's usually the bigger number, and it's the one nobody's tracking against quota."

Bridge — tied to what a sales leader specifically gets from AskCruz

"That's actually the exact problem AskCruz is built around for sales teams — it lets any rep ask a plain question and get an answer grounded in your actual account history, commitments, and inventory, instead of hunting across systems or waiting on the one person who remembers. For you specifically, that means faster quote turnaround, fewer broken promises, and new reps productive faster instead of leaning on your veterans for every routine question. Given what you just described, I think it's worth a real look."

Close, pick one based on what's still unclear

Confirm economic buyer: "Is this something you'd evaluate and decide on yourself, or does something like this need GM or ownership sign-off at your company?"
Confirm timeline: "Is this something you're actively trying to fix for your team right now, or more of a someday problem?"
Book time: "I'd like to show you how this would work against what you just described for your team — 20 minutes this week or next?"
