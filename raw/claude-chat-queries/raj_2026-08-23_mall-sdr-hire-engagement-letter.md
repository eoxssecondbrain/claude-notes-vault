Section 1 — Engagement structure and risk check

The core tension: what you asked for, and what you're actually setting up, work against each other.

What you asked for
Treat him as an independent contractor, invoiced corp-to-corp, no employment paperwork, no payroll hassle.

What the actual facts look like
- Paid hourly ($25/hr CAD) — payment for time, not for a deliverable. This is the single strongest signal courts and CRA look at.
- Full-time-sounding WFH role, starting tomorrow, calling on your behalf
- He'll operate on your infrastructure: askcruz.com email, your Chattr instance, your Claude account, your Zoom
- You're revamping his LinkedIn to represent AskCruz — that's you branding him as your rep, not as an external vendor
- You're handing him prompts and Company Brain access to learn your scripts, your jargon, your client list — that's direction and control over how the work gets done
- No mention of other clients — functionally exclusive to you
- He has no company yet. It doesn't exist. Which means the work he does starting tomorrow, before incorporation, is performed by him personally — you can't retroactively convert that into corp-to-corp after the fact.

Put together, this reads as a part-time employee with an invoice wrapper, not an independent contractor. The label in the engagement letter doesn't control the outcome — CRA (payroll tax assessment, penalties) and Ontario/provincial employment standards (notice, termination pay, if this ends badly) look at substance: control, tools, integration, exclusivity, chance of profit/risk of loss. On every one of those tests, this fact pattern points the same direction.

This isn't a paperwork nitpick. If CRA reclassifies him later, AskCruz/GFC owes back CPP/EI plus penalties. If he's let go and claims employee status, he can pursue termination pay under employment standards regardless of what the letter says.

The gap that makes it worse right now
He needs to incorporate before day one, not after. If he starts calling tomorrow and the company gets set up next week, that first stretch of work has no corp behind it — it's just him, personally, working for you hourly. Either push the start date to after incorporation, or accept that period is employment in substance and price that risk in.

Two honest paths, not one

Path A — Proceed as contractor-via-corp, mitigate what you can
- Require incorporation before day one, letter is between AskCruz/GFC and his corp only, never him personally
- Shift comp weight toward the per-meeting bonus and away from the hourly floor where possible — hourly-for-time is the worst-looking element
- Frame deliverables/output in the letter, not tracked hours, even if $25/hr is the internal calc you use to set expectations
- Short, explicit trial period with a clean exit clause — you have zero track record with this guy beyond one energetic conversation at a mall booth
- Accept you're carrying real, not eliminated, misclassification exposure. This reduces it, doesn't remove it — the branding/integration pieces you specifically want (askcruz.com identity, LinkedIn as your rep) cut directly against contractor status, and you're not going to give those up because they're the point of the hire.

Path B — Just make him casual/part-time payroll
More setup now (source deductions), but the exposure goes away. Worth considering if this SDR-from-street-hire pattern is one you intend to repeat — the risk compounds per person, not per incident.

One more thing worth naming plainly: you already run a structured Toronto SDR pipeline (61 applicants, hard disqualifiers, promotion path) and a 50-intern program with its own onboarding sequence. This hire bypassed all of that — no interview, no reference check, comp agreed on the spot after one conversation. That's not automatically wrong, but it's a deviation from process you built for a reason, and the trial/exit terms in the letter should compensate for the vetting you skipped, not just for the misclassification angle.
