## WEBSITE BLUEPRINT (15 sections)

Emotional arc: **Recognition → Tension → Clarity → Belief → Confidence → Action**

1. **Nav bar** — wordmark, 3–4 links, sticky CTA. Sparse nav = focused company.
2. **Hero** — headline describes THEIR reality; subhead makes the category claim. No carousel/autoplay. Target reaction: "how do they know that."
3. **Credibility strip** — highest-leverage 40px on the page; resolves the skepticism spike before it hardens into a bounce.
4. **The problem, articulated better than they could** — oblique enemy at work; also qualifies out poor-fit visitors, protecting delivery capacity.
5. **Why now** — why newly *possible*, not newly desirable. Removes shame about past failed software purchases.
6. **What it actually is** — plain paragraph + simple diagram. Confident people explain simply.
7. **How it works, 3 steps** — connect → ingest → ask. Honest about bespoke onboarding, framed as thoroughness.
8. **What you can ask it** — 5–8 real CEO-phrased questions. Highest-converting section. Use REAL client questions.
9. **What makes it different** — exactly 3. Handles "how is this different from ChatGPT" without asking it aloud.
10. **Proof and evidence** — placed AFTER differentiation.
11. **Objection handling** — security, data handling, integration, time required.
12. **Vision layer** — placed DEEP: read early it's hype, read late it's trajectory. Where investors scroll.
13. **Who this is for** — includes who it's NOT for. Protects the 30-onboarding constraint.
14. **Final CTA** — restated claim + what the call actually is (scoping, not pitch).
15. **Footer** — GFC relationship, contact, legal, privacy.

**Rules:** CTA minimum 3x (nav, after §8, final). Never before §4. Full page under 3 minutes.

## DESIGN / AESTHETIC SPEC

**Core correction:** Consumer luxury signals wealth through ornament; enterprise luxury signals it through restraint. If it looks like a watch brand, a CEO reads consultancy, not infrastructure. Target: quiet, dense, exact.

**Color** — Base #0B0C0E / #101114 (never pure black). Surfaces #16181C, #1E2126; elevation via background shift, not borders. Text #F2F3F5 / #9BA1A8 / #6B7178, never pure white. ONE accent, not gold: platinum #C8CDD4 or ember #C8642A, on under 5% of the page. Forbidden: multi-hue gradients, purple-blue AI gradient, glassmorphism, neon.

**Typography** — Two families max. Display: Söhne, Neue Haas Grotesk, GT America, or serif (Editorial New, Signifier). Serif headline + sans body is the most reliable "expensive" B2B move. Tracking -0.02 to -0.03em; line-height 1.6–1.7; body 17–19px; max 3 weights; tabular figures.

**Space** — Section padding 120–180px. Content ~1100–1200px, columns 65–70 chars. Asymmetry over centring. Empty space IS the luxury signal.

**Motion** — cubic-bezier(0.16, 1, 0.3, 1). Never linear/bounce. 400–700ms entrances, 150–250ms hover. Reveals: fade + 12–20px translate, 60–80ms stagger, trigger at 15% viewport. No scale-on-hover, no custom cursors. Only ~1/3 of sections animate. Respect prefers-reduced-motion.

**Graphics** — NO stock photography ever. (1) product as hero, cropped at edges [PRIMARY]; (2) 1px abstraction line work [connective tissue]; (3) real client-facility photography only if access is genuine.

**Texture** — 2–4% noise overlay. Soft colorless radial glow behind hero. Borders rgba white 6–8%. Radius 6–8px.

Ranking: maproom.ai > themaproom.ai > getmaproom.com > maproomhq.com > mymaproom.com.

**R6 CEREBRO / brain anatomy:** Disney is NOT the main risk. Cerebri AI Inc. holds CEREBRI / CEREBRI AI marks covering software for improving business processes via ML/NLP/data integration — identical class. CEREBRAS (S-1 April 2026). CEREBRO live/pending, Ontario, software class. Cerebriu (Denmark). "Cereb-" closed. Brain anatomy is the most-mined field in AI naming.

**R7 obscure real words, ALL COLLIDE:** Quipu (5x, incl. deep-tech startup nearly matching this product's spec), Orrery (Orrery.ai), Alidade, Gnomon, Portolan (German ERP company — worst collision), Ephemeris, Vernier, Exchequer, Palimpsest. Conclusion: "obscure word with a great reveal" is the standard playbook since Palantir (2003).

**R8 coined Latin REJECTED.** Raj: "no significance... I as a creator won't be able to remember. we need a name that stays with you once you hear it."
**DIAGNOSIS:** "makes you question what it means" and "stays with you once you hear it" fight each other. Obscure words provoke curiosity precisely because they have no foothold in memory — which is why they were available.
**REFRAMING INSIGHT:** The only name that ever stuck in this venture is *Ganesh Fruit Company*, named for Raj's grandfather's fifty-year fruit business. Significance from heritage and story, not etymology.
**MUNIM proposed** — the bookkeeper in a traditional Indian family business who held the entire operational memory. Raj LIKED THE DIRECTION; munim.com/.ai unavailable.

**R9 Indian-heritage options.** Ruled out: Daftar (means "list" in Indonesian/Malay — fighting a language for SEO), Karta (AI support company), Khata (Khatabook). Proposed: **Munshi** (the clerk who kept the books AND wrote all correspondence — the only role doing both, doubly apt since the product ingests email), Patwari, Kanungo, Bahi, **Paras** (the stone that turns iron to gold — easiest US pronunciation all session, one-sentence unforgettable story for a steel-forged company), Parakh, Kasauti, Sakshi, Lekha, Hisab, Hundi. Recommendation: Paras first, Munshi second.

**R10 American surnames / street names.**
*Street names steered off* — atmosphere, not meaning; borrows an identity that isn't Raj's; would repeat the "no significance" failure.
*Surnames work only when the person's life's work prefigured the product:* **Sanborn** (mapped every building in every American city, block by block — the most granular record of American commerce ever assembled), **Hollerith** (built the 1890 Census tabulator; his company became IBM; first man to make a nation's data machine-readable), **Deming** (taught management by measurement; a share of the ICP already knows the name — but Deming NM complicates the domain), **Bradstreet** (Lewis Tappan's Mercantile Agency, 1841, the first commercial business-intelligence network — but D&B collision).

**R11 — Raj chose this direction: name it after a great historical thinker, "like Claude was named after Claude Shannon."**

*Correction given:* Anthropic has never formally confirmed the origin of "Claude"; Shannon is the widely assumed source but it's speculation. The pattern is real regardless (Harvey, Watson, Ada, Alfred).

*Structural decision surfaced:* first names (Claude, Harvey, Watson) make the product feel like a person in the room — matches the interaction model and the locked advisor persona — but common first names are gone at any sane domain price. Surnames are more available and read more institutional. This choice changes the shortlist.

*Candidates whose actual work describes the product:*
- **Polanyi** — Michael Polanyi's tacit knowledge: "we know more than we can tell." The expertise running a business is real, operative, largely unwritten, and leaves when the person does. Raj's problem statement, written by a philosopher in 1958. po-LAN-yee.
- **Playfair** — William Playfair invented the line graph, bar chart and pie chart; before him numbers were columns you read, after him pictures you understood. Two syllables, and contains "fair" — an accidental trust signal.
- **Otlet** — Paul Otlet built the Mundaneum, indexing all world knowledge on millions of cards, and in the 1930s described a machine for reading any document remotely. Imagined this product seventy years early.
- **Tukey** — John Tukey coined "bit" and "software," invented exploratory data analysis: interrogate data to discover which questions are worth asking. Exactly how a CEO uses the product. TOO-key.
- **Ashby** — W. Ross Ashby's law of requisite variety: a controller must be at least as complex as what it controls. The business outgrew its tools, which is why the owner is blind.
- Higher recognition, worse availability: **Diderot** (compiled the Encyclopédie so knowledge could be used, not merely stored), **Leibniz** ("let us calculate").

**Claude's ranking:** Polanyi first (tacit knowledge is the most precise articulation of the product's reason to exist ever written), Playfair second (easiest to say; the "fair" accident is a gift), Tukey third.

**Caveat repeated:** this direction borrows someone else's significance rather than drawing on Raj's own. Polanyi is a magnificent intellectual fit but it isn't his grandfather. Both bets are legitimate — Raj should know which he's making.

Next step: check Polanyi, Playfair, Otlet, Tukey, Ashby on a registrar; expect at least two to be clear.
