Root cause, not just a motivation problem

Look at what's actually different between "study session" and everything else at EOXS: task completion, snippets, and productivity are scored with an explicit rubric (deadline weighting, percentage bands, points) — verified straight from the Aug 25 transcript. "Bring a new finding to study session" has no equivalent measurement. It's the only unscored, unstructured, fully voluntary block on the calendar. People rationally under-invest in the one thing nobody is counting.

Second factor, visible in the room composition: this group includes interns alongside Raj and you. An open-floor "anyone have anything?" format after 45 minutes of unstructured exploring puts the burden of volunteering on the least senior, least confident people in the room, in front of leadership. That's a confidence/safety problem, not a laziness problem, for at least some of the silent majority — worth not collapsing into one diagnosis.

What would actually change the pattern

Assign, don't open-floor. Before the 45-minute block, hand out 2-3 specific questions people must bring one answer to — not "explore however you want." Ron already tried something adjacent to this on Sept 1 (audit trail idea); make that the norm, not a one-off.

Rotate a mandatory presenter list. Each session, name 2-3 people (not always Ayan/Nidhi/Sheenam) who must report a finding first. Rotating the obligation is what breaks "same few people" — it's structural, not a personality trait of the quiet ones.

Score it like everything else. You already track task and snippet output to the point. Add a line for study-session contribution to that same visible system. If it's not measured, it's not real to people who are already being measured on everything else.

Shrink the room for the actual work. Keep the 45-minute exploration in pairs or trios instead of solo-then-full-group, then have pairs report out. Lower the bar to say something half-formed to one person before saying it to Raj and you.

One thing I'd push back on in the framing: "for their benefit also" is true but abstract — nobody changes behavior for an abstract benefit. If a finding from study session actually gets used in AskCruz's product or customer-facing work, say so out loud and credit that person by name. That's the concrete version of "benefit," and it's the one that compounds.
