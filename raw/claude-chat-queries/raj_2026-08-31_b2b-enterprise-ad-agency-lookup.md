Assistant: Three real candidates, verified against current (2026) client rosters and awards records — not agency self-description.

**Stein** (formerly Stein IAS)
The only pure-play specialist of the three — B2B is all they do. Named #1 B2B Agency in the World by The Drum Honors 2025, ANA B2B Agency of the Year 13 times in 15 years, and the only B2B agency to win consecutive WARC Gold Effectiveness awards. Clients include Celonis (process mining — closest analog to AskCruz's category), Juniper Networks, Wolters Kluwer, HCLTech, HSBC. Their model (brand + demand fused, not just creative) is built for exactly the long, technical, multi-stakeholder sales cycle steel ERP buying involves.

**BBDO**
SAP's creative agency of record since 2014 — an 11+ year run, not a one-off pitch win. Made "Unstoppable" and "Office Cat," both cited as 2025-26 standouts for making enterprise/AI software emotionally legible to non-technical buyers. Omnicom-network scale and production muscle; less B2B-only focus than Stein but proven at translating dense enterprise software into culture-level campaigns.

**Ogilvy**
32-year IBM AOR (Solutions for a Small Planet, Smarter Planet, Watson, Think) — the deepest enterprise-tech creative pedigree that exists, and IBM was the first B2B brand inducted into the Advertising Hall of Fame partly because of this work. Relevant right now: Ogilvy declined to defend the IBM account in March 2026 as part of a broader WPP restructuring (commercial, not creative, dispute) — they just came off their flagship enterprise-tech anchor and have bandwidth/incentive for a new one.

One blind spot worth flagging even though budget isn't your constraint: all three are built for $1B+ marketers — SAP's disclosed media spend alone is ~$17M/year, Stein's stated client floor is $1B+ revenue. None of them will right-size a $250K engagement the way a boutique would; you'd be buying their attention, not just their creative, and that's a relationship-management problem more than a money one. If AskCruz isn't yet at the deal size where a Fortune 500 CMO would get this agency on the phone, the realistic path in is usually through a senior individual at the agency (ECD/planning director) taking it as a passion project, not the agency's standard new-business process.
