Two separate stories emerge from the archive, and they don't fully agree, which is worth being upfront about.

The verifiable one (from Rajat Jain's own emails, primary source)

EOXS didn't start as EOXS. It started as The Steel Store, a B2B marketplace connecting steel manufacturers with production houses — bootstrapped, ~30% MoM growth, 85% retention, pitched to VCs (Unshackled Ventures, Basis Set Ventures, Amasia) in 2020. Basis Set passed specifically because a pure marketplace with a ~3% take rate would eventually force EOXS into capital-intensive direct buying/selling of steel to reach venture scale — not a fit for their model. That rejection reasoning turned out to be prophetic.

Rajat then took Mucker Capital's accelerator offer and did a pre-seed round with them. In his own words to a VC in June 2021: "We started with our marketplace but quickly came to the understanding that the stand alone Marketplace will not have enough stickiness. Therefore, we pivoted into providing technology to Steel Distributors. We build a SaaS ERP, Ecomm (think Shopify), CRM focused on Steel in 6 months." By that point they had done 30 demos and had 3 paying customers.

By October 2023 the pivot was complete and stated plainly to another investor: "we have pivoted to ERP from the Marketplace. We have explored marketplaces for Steel and followed companies like Reibus who have raised over $100M to scale but unfortunately are struggling due to lack of market adaptation. Steel Distributors still use blue screen software and there is a massive demand for modern technology."

So the core idea, in the founder's own contemporaneous words, was never "build an ERP." It was "bring technology to an industry that has none" — the marketplace was the first attempt at that, the ERP/CRM/e-commerce suite was the second, arrived at because the first didn't stick.

The secondhand one (from an internal 2021 "story of EOXS" writing exercise)

Around September 2021, roughly 20 interns/team members were given a task: write a story about EOXS, its mission, and why it's "a solution, not a software" — this looks like a candidate-screening or content exercise, not an official company history, and it shows: no two entries agree on what the acronym even stands for (End Of Exclusive Sphere, Enterprise of Operations, Enabling Opportunities through Extensive Solutions, ERP on Xpress Steel, "Entry of OXS"). That inconsistency itself tells you the acronym is retrofitted marketing, not a founding fact.

What is consistent across multiple independent entries in that document is the underlying narrative: Rajat Jain spent roughly a decade in the US steel industry, got frustrated that the industry still ran on paperwork and 1990s-era software while every other industry was modernizing, pushed his existing ERP vendor for improvements, got ignored, and decided to build it himself. One entry states he started with a team of 3 and built two failed early products before landing on what worked. Take this at the confidence level it deserves — it's a secondhand account written by interns, not Rajat, and none of it is corroborated by primary documents the way the Steel Store/Mucker/pivot story is.

Bottom line

The honest version: EOXS is a marketplace pivot, not a from-scratch ERP vision. The founder's core belief — steel distribution runs on outdated tools and there's real money in fixing that — stayed constant; the product he thought would prove it changed twice (marketplace → SaaS ERP+CRM+ecomm suite).
