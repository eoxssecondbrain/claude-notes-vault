Straight take, as a branding person:

What's actually good here: unsolicited third-party praise, forwarded casually, mentioning "pulling the plug on his existing investment" — that's a real signal, not a testimonial you fished for. That's rare and valuable. Screenshots of internal Slack/email threads work on LinkedIn specifically because they read as leaked truth, not marketing copy.

What's wrong with posting it as-is:

1. You didn't write it, Ryan did, about a prospect (Tripp), to you. Posting it publicly — even redacted — means you're publishing someone else's private correspondence about a third person who hasn't consented to being written about. Redacting names doesn't fully solve this: people in his circle (SMU, industry) may recognize the deal, the wording, or connect dots. If Tripp or Ryan ever sees this circulating, it reads as "Raj posts my private emails to sell." That's a trust cost bigger than the post's value.
2. Even fully clean, it's a screenshot of praise about a system, not a customer speaking in their own voice. Followers can't verify it. It reads as "look, someone likes us" rather than proof.

Better version of the same signal: don't post the screenshot. Extract the insight and either (a) get Tripp's or Ryan's explicit permission to quote them by name/company once the deal is real, or (b) write the post yourself, describing the pattern anonymized as a story ("Had a prospect tell a colleague this week they're ready to pull the plug on their existing investment and go 360 the other direction — after seeing what EOXS actually does day to day") without a screenshot at all. Same social proof, zero exposure, and it's in your voice which is what builds your personal brand, not Ryan's inbox.

If the deal closes, the actual power move is a named case study or quote with Tripp's permission — that's 10x the credibility of an anonymized screenshot and costs you nothing in trust.
