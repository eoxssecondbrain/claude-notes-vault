User: Give me 5 points which i need to look upon while i am working so that i can improve on myself how things can be done...

Assistant: This doesn't match eoxs-data-general (no EOXS data involved) and isn't a research/strategic task requiring rigor levels — it's a direct reflective question, so I'll just answer it.

Five things to watch while you work:

1. Are you doing the task or avoiding a harder one. Busywork feels productive but often exists to dodge the thing that actually moves the needle (a hard conversation, a decision, an unpleasant email). Check: if this task disappeared, would anything important stall?

2. Time spent vs. leverage generated. Hours logged isn't the metric. Ask what this hour did for sales, retention, or execution specifically — not "felt like work."

3. Decisions you're deferring by staying "busy." Indecision disguised as more research or more prep is still indecision. If you've had enough information for a while and haven't acted, that's the flag, not the task list.

4. Context-switching cost. Every switch between unrelated threads has a hidden tax — reloading context, losing momentum. If you're jumping between 5 things an hour, none of them are getting your best thinking.

5. Whether you're solving the actual problem or a symptom of it. Fixing today's fire without asking why it recurs means you'll be back here next week. Worth periodically stepping back and asking if this is a pattern.

The common thread: most self-improvement-at-work failures aren't about effort, they're about what you chose to spend the effort on. Track fewer things, but check that the ones you're tracking are the right ones.
