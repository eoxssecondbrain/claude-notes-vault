Assistant: Here's the first-round screening set for MBA Marketing Intern at AskCruz — 11 questions, conversational, ordered for a 5-10 minute call.

Suggested order and questions

1. Warm-up: "Tell me a bit about yourself — your MBA program, specialization, and which semester you're currently in."
Purpose: confirm eligibility basics (program, semester) and get a read on communication under an easy, low-pressure opener.
Strong answer: concise 30-60 second intro, states college/specialization/semester clearly, matches their resume.
Red flags: rambling with no structure, can't state their own semester clearly, resume mismatch.

2. "Why did you choose Marketing over other MBA specializations?"
Purpose: tests whether the interest is genuine or just a fallback choice.
Strong answer: personal reasoning tied to actual interests or strengths (creativity, people skills, consumer behavior), said with some energy.
Red flags: "marketing seemed easier than finance," no real reasoning, pure placement-driven answer.

3. "What made you apply here specifically, and what do you know about what AskCruz does?"
Purpose: checks if they actually researched the company or are mass-applying.
Strong answer: can describe AskCruz's business in their own words and connect it to why they want in.
Red flags: blank stare, "I just applied to a lot of places," zero company knowledge.

4. "How would you explain marketing, in simple terms, to someone who's never studied it?"
Purpose: basic conceptual grasp plus ability to communicate without jargon.
Strong answer: clear, plain-language explanation — creating value/awareness for a customer need.
Red flags: only textbook jargon with no real understanding underneath, visibly confused.

5. "In your own words, what's the difference between marketing and sales?"
Purpose: fundamentals check — a candidate heading into a marketing internship should have this cold.
Strong answer: marketing = building awareness/demand/positioning, sales = converting that into a purchase, with some nuance.
Red flags: no distinction at all, or clearly guessing.

6. "Tell me about any internship, project, or even a college activity where you did something marketing-related — could be a fest, a small business, a social media page you ran."
Purpose: relevant hands-on exposure and whether they take ownership of their work.
Strong answer: specific example, their actual role and actions, some result (even qualitative — "engagement went up," "we got 200 signups").
Red flags: vague, can't go past one sentence, always says "we" and never "I did."

7. "Say we launched something new tomorrow and gave you a small budget to promote it to college students — what would you actually do?"
Purpose: practical, real-world problem-solving under a constraint, not textbook theory.
Strong answer: structured thinking — who's the audience, which channel, why, respects the budget constraint.
Red flags: can't get past "post it on Instagram," no structure, ignores the budget/audience entirely.

8. "Give me one fun or catchy way you'd promote [AskCruz's product] on social media."
Purpose: creativity, on the spot.
Strong answer: an actual original idea suited to a platform/audience, said with some enthusiasm.
Red flags: total blank, or a generic recycled idea with no thought behind it.

9. "Have you worked with things like Instagram/LinkedIn content, SEO, or lead generation — even informally? Walk me through what you know."
Purpose: baseline digital marketing exposure, if relevant to this role.
Strong answer: some real exposure (ran a page, helped with a campaign, understands what a "lead" or "funnel" is) or at least sharp awareness and eagerness to learn.
Red flags: zero exposure and no curiosity about it either.

10. "This internship runs [X months] starting [date] — are you available for the full duration, and how many hours a day/week can you actually commit?"
Purpose: pure logistics — avoids wasting a slot on someone who can't follow through.
Strong answer: clear yes with hours specified, proactively flags any exam clashes or conflicts.
Red flags: vague/non-committal answers, conflicts they didn't mention until pressed.

11. Closing: "Why should we pick you for this over other candidates, and what do you want to get out of this internship?"
Purpose: overall motivation, self-awareness, and a genuine growth angle rather than just "I need credits."
Strong answer: specific, grounded strengths plus a real learning goal.
Red flags: hollow overconfidence, or the opposite — can't articulate any value at all.

Scoring framework

Rate the candidate 1-3 on each of these five dimensions right after the call (1 = weak, 2 = average, 3 = strong):

- Communication clarity (Q1, Q4, throughout)
- Motivation — for Marketing and for AskCruz specifically (Q2, Q3, Q11)
- Basic marketing understanding (Q4, Q5, Q9)
- Practical/creative thinking (Q6, Q7, Q8)
- Availability and commitment fit (Q10)

Total out of 15.

12-15: move to next round without hesitation.
8-11: borderline — worth a second look if you're short on candidates, but not a priority.
Below 8: don't advance.

One thing worth flagging separately from the scoring, since it's binary rather than a spectrum: if Q10 (availability) doesn't actually match what you need, everything else is moot — treat that as a hard filter before you even get to the total score, not just one of five inputs into it.
