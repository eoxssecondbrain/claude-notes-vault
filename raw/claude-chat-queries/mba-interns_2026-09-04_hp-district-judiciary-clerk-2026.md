## Assistant

Research findings delivered.

Timing: applications for Advt. DJ-ADVT./2026(1) dated 5 August 2026 close 10 September 2026, six days from the date of this conversation. Portal hphcrecruitment.in, fee ₹450 UR / ₹300 reserved HP.

Disambiguation: two separate HP High Court adverts ran in 2026 under different rule books — HC-Misc./2026(1) of 27 February for the High Court's own establishment (19 posts, R&P Rules 2015, closed March), and DJ-ADVT./2026(1) for the District Judiciary (388 posts, HP District Judiciary Staff Rules 2022, Schedule-III Part 7(A)). Warned against preparing from material written for the February advert.

Post: Clerk Group-C, 141 posts (84 regular, 57 contract). Graduation plus basic computer knowledge; typing 30 WPM English and 25 WPM Hindi in Kruti Dev-10. Age as on 1 January 2026: 18–45 UR/EWS, 18–50 reserved HP and HP government employees. Final-year candidates ineligible. Flagged one outlier source claiming 61 posts at 10+2, against every other source saying 141 and graduation.

Scheme:
- Screening Test — 100 marks, 2 hours, English, MCQ on OMR, one mark each, no negative marking. GK 70 (International 15, National 25, HP 30), Basic Computers 10, Reasoning 20. Shortlisting at 1:5.
- Written Test — 80 marks, 2 hours: English essay 500 words (25), Hindi essay 500 words (25), English-to-Hindi translation (15), Hindi-to-English translation (15). Qualifying 45%.
- Typing Test — 10 minutes, 30/25 WPM, 10% error ceiling, merely qualifying.

The decisive finding: the final merit list is drawn solely on written-test marks. The screening test carries zero merit weight — it is a gate, like a banking Prelims. Rank is decided entirely by an 80-mark subjective paper of two 500-word essays and two translations, which is the block almost no coaching material addresses because MCQs are easier to sell.

Competition: 141 vacancies at 1:5 means roughly 705 reach the written stage. No published cutoffs exist for previous HP High Court Clerk rounds — the sites promising cutoff data are placeholders with no figures. Plan to the ratio, not a mark: the screening target is a rank, not a score.

Strategy given: HP GK at 30 marks is the largest single block and the only part no all-India material covers, including the explicitly listed Indian and HP judiciary questions that standard GK books skip. Reasoning (20) and Computer (10) transfer almost completely from the RRB PO work already done. Noted that there is no quantitative aptitude anywhere in this syllabus, so none of the DI or arithmetic preparation serves this exam. Essay and translation practice must start now rather than after the screening result, since that paper decides selection and translation improves measurably over six weeks and not at all over six days. Kruti Dev-10 Hindi typing flagged as a distinct non-phonetic skill that candidates routinely underestimate on a purely qualifying stage.

Risks flagged: the 57 contractual posts carry different service terms from the 84 regular ones; the rules permit dispensing with the screening test if applications are low; the exam date is unannounced.

Closing challenge: two live exam tracks now run alongside a company with a $1M ARR target. RRB Mains is 20 December and this screening probably lands in the same window. Both cannot be prepared properly at once, and HP Clerk is a Group-C state post against RRB Officer Scale-I as a gazetted banking officer post — different ceilings, so the choice should be made deliberately rather than by drift.
