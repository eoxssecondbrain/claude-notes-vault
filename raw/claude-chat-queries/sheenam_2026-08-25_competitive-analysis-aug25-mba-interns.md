Assistant summarized companies reviewed with verdicts:
1. Bravo AI — ERP-like, no real AI differentiation, not a threat.
2. C3 AI — founded 2009, enterprise AI/ML for manufacturing/energy/defense (US Army/Navy/Air Force/Shell clients), 3.5/10, different space.
3. Cognito — founded 2020, Mountain View, $30M raised (Khosla), NL-driven enterprise workflow automation (SAP/Oracle/Workday/Salesforce), moderate but not direct threat, not steel-specific.

6. Rolio.ai — task-executing agent (returns/refunds under threshold, claims agent "Stan"), 5.5/10, executes vs. AskCruz's inform-and-empower model.
7. Endeavor AI — builds custom proprietary AI per client (consulting+product hybrid), flagged as closer analog, worth Ron's further review.
8. Dust AI — platform to build your own AI agents/automations (works with ChatGPT/Claude) rather than selling a built agent; flagged as worth watching as AskCruz's product expands.
9. Catalan — early-stage (2025) co-pilot for chemical distributors, very similar model to AskCruz but vertical-limited; clean minimal website flagged as design reference.
10. Ignite — document/content governance platform, ~3/10, not a competitor.
11. AskSaif/AskCypher-type site (Ron's find) — near-identical front-end/positioning to AskCruz, closest match found so far.

Recurring themes: most "competitors" are ERPs/automation tools with thin AI layers; key distinguishing question is information-provision (AskCruz's model) vs. autonomous task execution; websites reviewed are overcomplex, used as negative design lesson; naming consistency ("AskCruz" not "Cruz") flagged to discuss with Raj.

Next steps: interns to spend half of the next day researching more competitors using broadened sources; reconvene at 9 AM IST the next day on the same call link to review findings; team to discuss consistent "AskCruz" branding with Raj before site/blog goes live.
