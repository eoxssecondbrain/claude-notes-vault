# Toronto SDR/AE Comp Benchmark

**User:** If I have to hire an experienced and hungry SDR in Toronto to sell in the US market, we hire him as an AE, entry level. How much money am I looking at giving him?

**Assistant:** This doesn't clearly match either of your defined skills — it's not EOXS vault data (raj-eoxs-vault) and not deal/GTM strategy (sales-strategist). It's an external comp-benchmarking question, so I'll treat it as general research rather than force-fit a skill.

One clarification first: "hungry SDR-caliber person, hired as entry-level AE" — comp structures differ a lot between SDR and AE roles (SDR is usually lower base/OTE, AE has quota-carrying comp with higher variable). Also, "Toronto SDR selling into the US market" often gets priced closer to US-market-adjusted rates by companies that want US-quality talent, not local Toronto rates. I want to nail the target before I quote a number.

Pick a rigor level for the comp research:

- **5ft deep** — Quick market range from general knowledge/aggregate data (Glassdoor/Levels-type averages for Toronto entry-level AE, tech/B2B SaaS)
- **10ft deep** — Verified: cross-check 2-3 actual sources (comp surveys, job postings for Toronto-based AEs selling to US, recruiting firm benchmarks) rather than relying on aggregate averages
- **50ft deep** — Deep: verified + adjacent angles — e.g. CAD vs USD comp expectations, base/variable split norms, equity/ESOP substitution given EOXS's stage, what "entry-level AE" actually gets you vs a proper SDR, and risk of underpaying vs market given US-quota expectations

Which level?

**User:** 50

**Assistant:** Bottom line: CAD $55K–$65K base, $90K–$115K OTE, 55/45 to 60/40 base-heavy split. Here's the reasoning and sourcing.

Toronto market data (verified across sources):

| RepVue | AE, Canada (all levels) | $105K CAD median | $198K CAD median OTE |

The Glassdoor/Wellfound/RepVue numbers are all-level blends dominated by tenured reps at bigger companies — they overstate what an entry-level hire should get. Payscale's entry-level cut and the SDR benchmarks are the more relevant floor.

Where your candidate actually sits: You're describing an experienced SDR being leveled up into an entry-level AE seat. That's not a pure "entry-level AE" (someone with zero sales background) — it's someone with proven prospecting chops taking on their first quota. Price them above the pure Payscale entry-level AE floor ($44K median) but below a market-rate AE with closing experience ($97K+ blended average). That puts base in the $55K–$65K CAD range, roughly matching an experienced SDR base plus a step-up for the AE title and quota responsibility.

On the split: Standard B2B SaaS AE split is 50/50 (Warp, Fullcast, Talentfoot all converge here), but that assumes an established playbook and a real quota built from historical data. EOXS doesn't have that for this hire — no proven US-outbound-to-close motion for this specific role yet. The "founding AE" comp pattern is the more honest comparable here: 60/40 or 55/45 base-heavy, because you're asking someone to help figure out the motion, not just execute a known one. Don't back into an aggressive quota just to justify a lower base — that's how you get 60%+ miss rates and turnover.

Three things that materially affect the number, beyond what you asked:

1. Currency mismatch. If this person sells into the US market, their deals are presumably USD-denominated (or at least USD-benchmarked). Paying 100% CAD comp — especially the variable piece — means their upside doesn't track the actual market they're selling into. Consider indexing commission to USD deal value even if base stays CAD.

2. Retention risk after they prove themselves. An "experienced and hungry" SDR who closes a few US deals in an entry-level AE seat becomes a legitimately marketable AE within 6–12 months. If you price at the low end of this range without a clear comp ladder (defined next-level base/OTE, not vague promises), you're training someone else's mid-market AE. Decide the 12-month step-up now, not when they ask for it.

3. No established quota = no defensible commission rate yet. Standard SaaS AE commission is 8–12% of new ARR at quota, set against a 4–5x quota-to-OTE ratio. You don't have deal-cycle data to set a real quota for this role at EOXS. Cleanest fix: start with a lower, semi-guaranteed variable component (ramped over the first 2 quarters) rather than a full commission plan built on guessed numbers — same logic as the "no quota at all, straight commission on closed deals" pattern used for genuine founding-AE hires.

One open question that changes the number: is this an EOXS hire (selling ERP) or a Krinox/GFC hire? If it's Krinox, given GFC's stage, the founding-AE framing applies even more literally, and equity/ESOP substitution for some cash becomes a live option worth considering.

**User:** I want to find someone who has two years of SDR experience and is hungry to become an AE. I want to find three of them in Toronto.

Tell me what do I need to do on my LinkedIn account to start this process?

Mission: let's build a Sales army.

My friend just invested $200,000 in the company and her only clause is that this needs to go into Sales

**Assistant:** Sales Navigator will work for this, but know its real limits before you commit: no "open to work" signal, no candidate pipeline/ATS, and InMail to people outside your network draws from a shared credit pool, not unlimited. It's built for prospecting buyers, not candidates — recruiters use it as a workaround because it's $99/mo vs LinkedIn Recruiter Lite at $170+/mo. For finding 3 people, Sales Nav is the right call. If this becomes a recurring hiring motion (which "sales army" implies), revisit Recruiter Lite later.

Setup steps:

1. Whose account. Sales Navigator licenses attach to an individual profile, not a company page. Decide: your own profile (CEO outreach lands harder — people respond to founders) or a team member's (less noisy, more scalable if you're doing this repeatedly). Given you have ~20K connections and this is a one-time push for 3 hires, use your own.

2. Subscribe to Sales Navigator Core ($99.99/mo, usually a free trial available). Don't buy Advanced/Advanced Plus tiers — you don't need team features for this.

3. Build the search:
   - Keywords: "SDR" OR "Sales Development Representative" OR "BDR"
   - Geography: Toronto, Ontario, Canada (or Greater Toronto Area)
   - Years in current position: 2 years (closest proxy Sales Nav has — it doesn't have Recruiter's exact "years of experience" filter, so also add "Years in current company" as a second pass to catch people who were promoted internally)
   - Industry: Computer Software / Information Technology / SaaS
   - Optional: add "quota" or "President's Club" or "top performer" to keywords to surface self-identified high performers

4. Save the search and turn on alerts — new matches get pushed to you as people update profiles or new SDRs enter Toronto market.

5. Outreach. You get 50 InMail credits/month on Core. For 3 hires, budget on roughly 10-15% response rate for cold recruiting messages — meaning you likely need to message 25-40 people to get 3 real conversations, let alone 3 hires. Personalize each message: mention EOXS, that it's a real AE seat (not a lateral SDR move), selling into the US market, and a clear comp range so you're not wasting cycles on mismatched expectations.

6. Track it outside LinkedIn. Sales Nav has no pipeline view — use a simple spreadsheet (name, stage, last contact, next step) or route it through your own CRM.

One thing worth flagging before you commit the $200K to this: 3 hires at the $55K–65K CAD base range from our last conversation is roughly $180K CAD in base salary alone for year one, before Ontario payroll burden (CPP/EI/EHT, ~8-12% on top), tools, onboarding, or the 4-6 month ramp period before an ex-SDR is actually producing as an AE. That's close to the full $200K before a single dollar of commission gets paid out — and commission is the part that's self-funding by definition, so it's not the risk. The risk is base + burden + ramp time eating the whole investment before you see quota-carrying output. Worth deciding now whether "goes into Sales" means 3 full hires immediately, or 1-2 hires plus a longer runway buffer.
