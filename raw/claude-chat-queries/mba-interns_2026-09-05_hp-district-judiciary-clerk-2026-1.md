Timing: Advt. DJ-ADVT./2026(1) dated 5 August 2026; applications close 10 September 2026. Portal hphcrecruitment.in; fee ₹450 UR / ₹300 reserved HP.

Disambiguation: two separate 2026 HP High Court adverts under different rule books — HC-Misc./2026(1) of 27 February for the High Court establishment (19 posts, R&P Rules 2015, closed March), and DJ-ADVT./2026(1) for the District Judiciary (388 posts, HP District Judiciary Staff Rules 2022, Schedule-III Part 7(A)).

Post: Clerk Group-C, 141 posts (84 regular, 57 contract). Graduation plus basic computer knowledge; typing 30 WPM English, 25 WPM Hindi in Kruti Dev-10. Age as on 1 January 2026: 18–45 UR/EWS, 18–50 reserved HP and HP government employees. Final-year candidates ineligible. One outlier source claiming 61 posts at 10+2 was flagged against the majority.

Scheme:
- Screening Test — 100 marks, 2 hours, English, MCQ on OMR, one mark each, no negative marking. GK 70 (International 15, National 25, HP 30), Basic Computers 10, Reasoning 20. Shortlisting 1:5.
- Written Test — 80 marks, 2 hours: English essay 500 words (25), Hindi essay 500 words (25), English-to-Hindi translation (15), Hindi-to-English translation (15). Qualifying 45%.
- Typing Test — 10 minutes, 30/25 WPM, 10% error ceiling, merely qualifying.

Decisive finding: the final merit list is drawn solely on written-test marks. The screening carries zero merit weight. Rank is decided by an 80-mark subjective paper that almost no coaching material addresses.

Competition: 141 vacancies at 1:5 gives roughly 705 reaching the written stage. No published cutoffs exist for previous rounds. Plan to the ratio, not a mark.

Also established: there is no quantitative aptitude anywhere in this syllabus, so RRB DI and arithmetic work does not transfer; reasoning and computer do.

Structural basis: two parallel tracks from day one, because the skill deciding rank (writing) takes months while the skill deciding entry (screening MCQs) takes weeks. Surfaced a rule most candidates miss — the typing test may be held on the same day as the written examination, so typing must be exam-ready before the screening result, not after.

Five phases with exit conditions:
- Phase 0, this week to 10 Sept: submit application; record three baselines — a 100-question screening mock, one 500-word essay in each language under 30 minutes, and a typing test in both languages with WPM and error rate.
- Phase 1, weeks 1–8: HP GK foundation, writing habit, typing from zero. Exit at first GK pass complete and 20 WPM English.
- Phase 2, weeks 9–12: current affairs layering, screening mocks. Exit at 65+ in a full mock.
- Phase 3, final three weeks: screening-only revision, exit at consistent 70+.
- Phase 4, screening to written exam: all-in on essay, translation and typing.

Re-anchoring rule: count backwards from the announced date — Phase 3 takes the last three weeks, Phase 2 the four before, Phase 1 absorbs the remainder; under eight weeks, keep only HP GK and the writing habit.

Daily template (2h30): HP GK 45 min, national and international current affairs 30, reasoning 20, computer 10, writing 25, typing 20. Weekends add a timed 500-word essay and a fortnightly full mock. Extra hours go only to GK and writing, never to reasoning or computer, which are already over-served by existing RRB material.

HP GK themes to rotate: history and princely states; geography (rivers, passes, valleys, districts); culture, fairs and temples; economy, horticulture and hydropower; state schemes and current HP affairs; and HP administrative and judicial structure — the last being the theme standard HP GK books omit while the rules explicitly place Indian and HP judiciary questions inside General Knowledge.

Writing track: essay topic bank oriented to a court recruitment (case pendency and judicial reform, e-courts, legal aid, women's safety law, HP tourism carrying capacity, hill disaster management, the drug problem in HP, unemployment and migration, Himalayan glaciers, digital literacy). Same topic in English one week and Hindi the next; 60% of writing time to the weaker language identified in Phase 0. Structure drilled to four-line opening, three body paragraphs each with one claim and one example, five-line close, written to word count. Translation days use editorial paragraphs in both directions with a running glossary of administrative and legal vocabulary. Target 55–62 of 80, not the 36 that merely qualifies.

Typing track: Kruti Dev is not phonetic and takes six to eight weeks from cold. Targets 15 WPM by week 3, 22 by week 6, 30 by week 10. Error rate tracked as seriously as speed — 35 WPM at 12% errors fails while 30 WPM at 6% passes.

Checkpoint table with remedial action for each miss.

Screening-day tactics: no negative marking, so attempting all 100 questions is strictly dominant; order computer, then reasoning, then GK; two hours for 100 questions favours accuracy over pace, unlike the banking Mains.
