# Podar/Krinox Website — Messaging Council & Naming

## Session purpose
Build a "council" to develop messaging and positioning for the website of GFC's flagship AI product (originally "Podar", briefly "Krinox", name still unresolved).

---

## Council composition
Facilitator/critic (Claude) plus five specialist seats: Category Positioning Strategist; B2B SaaS Messaging Strategist (Dunford school); ICP/Buyer Psychology Expert; Website Conversion Strategist; Brand Voice & Copy Director.

Operating model: Emotive Brand (SF B2B brand strategy agency). Principles applied:
1. "Why" before "what" and "how" — articulate why the status quo is broken, ignite belief, before explaining product.
2. Research-led, not founder-assumed — Raj already holds this asset (Fireflies/Fathom transcripts, email archives in OV).
3. Rational + emotional bond — clarity alone doesn't move buyers; belief does.
4. Emotional Audit — internal team belief before external messaging.

---

## Discovery answers (Raj)

- **Audience:** Both enterprise prospects and investors, dual narrative on one site.
- **Positioning:** Broad — "AI operating system for businesses" per GFC charter.
- **Proof points today:** IRIS live + Sabre SA2 pilot + internal EOXS use.
- **ICP:** Scenario A — steel is the current proof, but ICP was always meant to be broader/horizontal.
- **Buyer persona (refined):** CEO/Owner of a 50–100 employee mid-market company. The P&L owner. Culturally mainstream, not steeped in mythology or niche fiction.
- **Product status:** Fully built, ready to launch. Onboarding still requires bespoke integration work per client (like Sabre's build).
- **Revenue goal:** $1M in 3 months — ~30 customers at ~$30K ACV. Distinct from EOXS's $1M ARR/12mo goal.
- **Sales cycle:** Weeks. Fast-moving, decisive buyers.
- **Delivery capacity:** Plan is to hire/scale — NOT yet done.
- **Emotional core:** exhaustion from running on gut feel + frustration at flying blind despite being capable + wanting more control. Synthesized as: competence undermined by bad information, and a craving to feel in command again.
- **Persona:** Trusted senior advisor (buyer lacks visibility, not drive; advisor tone flatters competence rather than competing with the CEO's self-image).
- **Enemy:** Named obliquely, never explicitly. Describe the fragmented data reality so specifically that the inadequacy of alternatives is self-evident.

## Council decisions locked

**CTA:** Not a waitlist (too passive for a built product chasing $900K in 90 days), not open self-serve (bespoke integration is real). Correct CTA: "Book a fit call" — qualification/scoping, not queue-joining.

**Proof strategy:** "Steel as credibility weapon." Built and proven inside one of the most fragmented, custom, undocumented industries in American manufacturing — framed as evidence of range, not a limitation.

**Draft hero copy (v1):**
> You built something real. Most of what you know about it still lives in your head, one employee's inbox, and a system nobody fully trusts.
>
> [Name] is the senior advisor that's actually read everything — every order, every call, every email — and tells you the truth about your business before you have to ask.
>
> [Book a fit call]

## Website blueprint (15 sections)

Arc: Recognition → Tension → Clarity → Belief → Confidence → Action.

1. Nav — wordmark, 3–4 links, sticky CTA. Sparse nav signals focus; lets a buyer convinced at 30% scroll act instantly.
2. Hero — headline describes their situation not the product; subhead makes the category claim. Recognition moment.
3. Credibility strip — highest-leverage 40px on the page; resolves the skepticism spike before it hardens into a bounce.
4. Problem, articulated better than they could — the oblique enemy at work; also self-qualifies out poor-fit visitors.
5. Why now — newly possible, not newly desirable. Removes shame about past software failures.
6. What it actually is — plain paragraph + simple diagram. Deliberate plainness differentiates in a jargon-heavy category.
7. How it works, three steps — connect → ingest → ask. Honest about bespoke onboarding, framed as thoroughness.
8. What you can ask it — 5–8 real CEO-language questions. Highest-converting section; triggers self-insertion.
9. What makes it different — 3 differentiators. Handles "how is this different from ChatGPT" without asking it aloud.
10. Proof and evidence — steel narrative, client story, quote. After differentiation, because proof means more once they know what's being proven.
11. Objection handling — security, data handling, integration, time. Critical: you're asking for access to every email, call and transaction.
12. Vision layer — Company Brain → Hands → Digital Workforce. Placed deep: read early it's hype, read late it's trajectory. Investors scroll for this.
13. Who this is for — including who it's not for. Raises perceived value; operationally protects the 30-onboarding constraint.
14. Final CTA — restated claim + what the call actually is (scoping, not pitch).
15. Footer — entity, contact, legal, privacy. Trust signal checked more than founders assume.

**Rules:** CTA minimum 3x (nav, post-"what you can ask it", final). Never before the problem section. Whole page reads in under 3 minutes.

## Visual direction

**Core correction:** Consumer luxury (gold, ornament, gloss) and enterprise luxury (restraint, space, precision) are opposite. If it looks like a watch brand, a CEO reads consultancy, not infrastructure. Target: quiet, dense, exact. References: Linear, Vercel, Stripe, Palantir.

- **Color:** Near-black base #0B0C0E/#101114, never pure black. Surfaces #16181C, #1E2126 — elevation via background shift, not borders. Text #F2F3F5 / #9BA1A8 / #6B7178, never pure white. Exactly one accent: cold platinum #C8CDD4 or ember #C8642A, on under 5% of the page.
- **Type:** Two families max. Display: Söhne, Neue Haas Grotesk, GT America, or Editorial New/Signifier for a serif move. Body: Inter, Söhne, Suisse International. Headline tracking -0.02 to -0.03em. Body 17–19px, line-height 1.6–1.7. Max three weights. Tabular figures for all numbers.
- **Space:** Section padding 120–180px. Content ~1100–1200px, text columns 65–70 chars. Asymmetry over centring.
- **Motion:** cubic-bezier(0.16, 1, 0.3, 1). 400–700ms entrances, 150–250ms hover. Reveals fade + 12–20px translate, 60–80ms stagger, trigger at 15% into viewport. No scale-on-hover. Only ~1/3 of sections animate. Respect prefers-reduced-motion.
- **Graphics:** No stock photography. Primary: product as hero, real queries, cropped at section edges. Secondary: 1px line work specific to the actual architecture.
- **Texture:** 2–4% noise overlay. Soft colourless radial glow behind hero. Borders rgba white 6–8%. Radius 6–8px.
- **Forbidden:** gold/metallic gradients; purple-blue AI gradient; glassmorphism; stock photography; bounce easing; custom cursors; scale-on-hover; multiple accents; pure black/white; centred-everything.

## Naming — full history

**Krinox rejected** — Raj kept forgetting it himself. Treated as fatal. krinox.com purchase (planned 27 July) superseded. Prior vault page "Podar Naming Decision — Krinox" now obsolete.

### Round 1 — rejected as "not memorable"
Reframe: The Watcher (Uatu) is boring because his defining trait is non-intervention. The product does the opposite. Target archetype is a narrator, not a watcher.
Candidates: Sanjaya, Vidura, Chitragupta, Vyasa, Mimir, Muninn, Kvasir, Seldon, Varys, Mycroft, Demerzel, MU/NB/KV.

### Round 2 — rejected as "not catchy"
Raj cited Odin, Zeus, Sage as target register.
- **Sage — DEAD.** Sage Group is a multi-billion-pound accounting/ERP software company. Fatal in his category.
- **Zeus — compromised.** Famous banking trojan.
- **Odin — survives, only generically crowded.**
Candidates: Norn, Metis, Scry, Maat, Rune, Thoth, Ken, Vigil, Nabu.

### Round 3 — famous/mainstream brief
Point raised: Odin survived three rounds in Raj's head; nothing else did. Memorability beats uniqueness for founder-led selling of 30 deals.
Candidates: Horus, Merlin, Argo, Neo, Raven, Juno, Orion, Vulcan, Mentat.

### Round 4 — Raven proposed by Raj, then re-resolved
Raj's concept (retained as narrative): Three-Eyed Raven from GoT. An eye into the business that sees and knows everything; knows past and present, predicts the future. Maps to a three-part architecture — past (historical ERP data), present (live ingestion), future (forecasting).

**Crowding check run. Raven is the most contested name evaluated, with collisions inside the category:**
- **Raven (Y Combinator, Bengaluru, 2021)** — AI assistants for manufacturing operations: troubleshooting, job planning, reporting, real-time analytics, integrating with existing systems. Nearly Raj's product description, adjacent buyer, India-based team. Highest confusion risk.
- **Raven Intelligence (ravenintel.com)** — review site for ERP implementation partners; publishes on SAP, Unit4, ERP as "system of record" → "system of reasoning." Directly in Raj's industry media space.
- **Raven.io** — $20M March 2026, led by Norwest and Elron, runtime application security, SF, ~23 people. Holds the obvious domain.
- Ravenna.ai ($15M, Madrona/Khosla, AI-native ITSM); Raven360/Revinova ($12M, enterprise AI learning); Raven.build (AI CAD); Raven Software (Activision).

Earlier "crowding barely matters at deal four" advice explicitly retracted for this case — it fails when a same-named competitor sells AI assistants to manufacturing plants.

**Resolution proposed:** Name it Odin, keep the raven. Odin natively contains the Three-Eyed Raven story and predates it — Huginn and Muninn (thought and memory) fly the world daily and report back (the ingestion layer); Odin traded an eye at Mimir's well for knowledge of past, present and future. Name = Odin; visual identity = raven mark (better logo, works monochrome on the near-black palette); copy = the raven story; architecture = past/present/forecast. Alternative if he wants the bird as the name: Corvus.

### Round 5 — ICP-calibrated brief (current)
Raj's brief: easy to remember, movie or famous history reference, something CEOs would know, still connected to meaning. ICP specified as CEOs of 50–100 employee companies.

**Self-correction issued:** Every prior round named the product for Claude, not for the buyer. Sanjaya, Mimir, Kvasir, Metis, Mentat — a 58-year-old CEO of a 70-person distribution business has encountered none of them. The bar is not recognition; it's whether he understands why the name was chosen within one second, without explanation.

Candidates presented:
- **Radar** — plain English word for detecting what you can't see, plus Radar O'Reilly from M*A*S*H, who knew everything before it happened and told the commander first. Lands twice, needs no explanation. Best fit for this ICP.
- **Hubble** — the instrument that let people finally see what had always been there.
- **Sherlock** — sees what everyone missed in the same evidence. Association is competence, not magic.
- **Columbo** — finds truth by noticing the one thing that doesn't add up. Warmer, less intimidating than Sherlock.
- **Galileo** — looked through a lens and told the truth even when unwelcome.
- **Alfred** — runs the house, knows everything, tells Bruce what he doesn't want to hear. Reads as service rather than surveillance, which helps the data-access objection.
- **Magellan** — mapped the whole thing first; meaning transfers a beat slower.
Flagged not recommended: Yoda (sounds like a toy next to a $30K PO), Morpheus (half the buyers blank on the name).
Known crowding: Radar has common-word usage including a location SDK; Galileo collides with SoFi's payments processor; Sherlock used by Apple long ago; Alfred is a small Mac utility. None category-fatal.
Top three: Radar, Hubble, Sherlock.

### Round 6 — Yoda checked, RULED OUT ON LEGAL GROUNDS
Raj asked for domain availability on Yoda. Domain question is moot — the name is legally unavailable.
- YODA is a registered trademark of Lucasfilm Ltd. LLC (Disney), registrations dating from 1982 across many classes.
- Disney holds 5,000+ active registrations and is described as a "trademark hound."
- Attorneys addressing this exact question note use even on unrelated goods can violate 15 USC 1125(c) as dilution by blurring, because Yoda is a distinctive mark tied to a famous character. Every outside attempt to register YODA has been abandoned.
- Logic: Yoda isn't a real word, so the only reason to pick it is the Star Wars association — which is what makes it infringement rather than coincidence.
- Risk: building brand, domain and eventually a funded company on a name Disney can take at any point. Rebranding at customer thirty is far more expensive than choosing differently now.
- Secondary objection (independent of law): Yoda reads as whimsy next to a $30K purchase order. Radar and Sherlock carry authority.

**Generalized rule established:** Studio-owned character names carry the same class of risk — Columbo is Universal's, Alfred is Warner's, smaller only because those studios are less aggressive. The ownable categories are real words, public-domain characters, and mythological references: Radar, Hubble, Odin, Galileo, Sherlock (Conan Doyle works entered US public domain in 2023).

**Standing recommendation: Radar.** Say it on a cold call and no one asks what it means.
