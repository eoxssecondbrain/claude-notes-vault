# Podar/Krinox Website — Messaging Council & Naming

## Session purpose
Build a "council" to develop messaging and positioning for the website of GFC's flagship AI product (originally "Podar", briefly renamed "Krinox", now under renewed naming review).

---

## Council composition
Facilitator/critic (Claude) plus five specialist seats:
- Category Positioning Strategist
- B2B SaaS Messaging Strategist (Dunford/"Obviously Awesome" school)
- ICP / Buyer Psychology Expert
- Website Conversion Strategist
- Brand Voice & Copy Director

Operating model adopted: Emotive Brand (SF-based B2B brand strategy agency). Key principles pulled from their published methodology:
1. "Why" before "what" and "how" — articulate why the status quo is broken and ignite belief before explaining product.
2. Research-led, not founder-assumed — one-on-one research with customers, prospects, lost deals. Raj already holds this asset (Fireflies/Fathom transcripts, email archives in OV).
3. Rational + emotional bond — clarity alone doesn't move buyers; belief does. Emotion is the accelerant in enterprise decisions, not just consumer.
4. Emotional Audit — assess internal team belief before external messaging.

**Audience:** Both — dual narrative on one site (enterprise prospects + investors).
**Original CTA choice:** Waitlist (later overturned, see below).
**Positioning:** Broad — "AI operating system for businesses" per GFC charter.
**Proof points today:** IRIS live + Sabre SA2 pilot + internal EOXS use (three stacked).
**First real client live:** Within the next month.
**ICP:** Scenario A — steel is the current proof, but ICP was always meant to be broader/horizontal. Site speaks horizontally now, ahead of the tech, because the ambition/fundraising story requires it.
**Buyer persona:** CEO/Owner of a mid-market company — the P&L owner.
**Product status:** Fully built, ready to launch. BUT onboarding still requires bespoke integration work per client (like Sabre's build).
**Revenue goal:** $1M in 3 months for this product — ~30 customers at ~$30K ACV. (Distinct from EOXS's $1M ARR/12mo goal — do not conflate.)
**Sales cycle:** Weeks — fast-moving, decisive buyers.
**Delivery capacity:** Plan is to hire/scale delivery fast enough — NOT yet done.

**Emotional core (Raj's answer):** exhaustion from running on gut feel + frustration at flying blind despite being capable + wanting more control over the business. Synthesized by council into a single arc: competence undermined by bad information, and a craving to feel in command again.

**Persona + enemy decisions delegated to Claude:**
- Persona: Trusted senior advisor (not relentless operator, not bold pioneer). Reasoning: buyer is already capable and fast-moving; what they lack is visibility, not drive. Advisor tone flatters competence instead of competing with the CEO's self-image.
- Enemy: Named obliquely, never explicitly. Don't call out chatbots/competitors by name — describe the buyer's fragmented data reality with such specificity that the inadequacy of alternatives is self-evident. Belief through recognition, not argument.

## Council decisions locked

**CTA resolution:** Not a waitlist (too passive for a built product chasing $900K in 90 days), not open self-serve (bespoke integration is real). Correct CTA: "Book a fit call" — framed as qualification/scoping, not queue-joining.

**Proof-point strategy:** Option 1 — "steel as credibility weapon." State plainly the product was built and proven inside one of the most fragmented, custom, undocumented industries in American manufacturing. Reframes the narrow proof as evidence of range, not a limitation to hide.

**Draft hero copy (v1, written when name was still Krinox):**
> You built something real. Most of what you know about it still lives in your head, one employee's inbox, and a system nobody fully trusts.
>
> [Name] is the senior advisor that's actually read everything — every order, every call, every email — and tells you the truth about your business before you have to ask.
>
> [Book a fit call]

Proof line beneath:
> Built and proven inside one of the most fragmented, custom industries in American manufacturing. If it can bring order to that, it can bring order to yours.

## Website blueprint (15 sections)

Emotional arc the page executes: Recognition → Tension → Clarity → Belief → Confidence → Action.

1. **Navigation bar** — wordmark, 3–4 links max, persistent CTA button, sticky. Sparse nav signals focus; persistent CTA lets a buyer convinced at 30% scroll act instantly.
2. **Hero** — one headline (describes their situation, not the product), one subhead (the category claim), primary CTA, secondary "see how it works" link. Recognition moment. No carousel, no autoplay video.
3. **Credibility strip** — thin band directly under hero. Highest-leverage 40px on the page: resolves the skepticism spike a bold claim creates, before it hardens into a bounce.
4. **The problem, articulated better than they could** — 3–4 concrete descriptions of fragmented reality. The oblique enemy at work. Also self-qualifies out poor-fit visitors, protecting delivery capacity.
5. **Why now** — why this is newly possible, not newly desirable. Removes shame about past software failures; creates urgency without fake scarcity.
6. **What it actually is** — one plain paragraph + simple diagram. Analytical brain re-engages and demands mechanism. Deliberate plainness is itself a differentiator in a jargon-heavy category.
7. **How it works, three steps** — connect → ingest → ask. Honest about bespoke onboarding, framed as thoroughness. Shrinks the buyer's private simulation of ERP-rollout pain; pre-frames the fit call as necessary, not a gate.
8. **What you can ask it** — 5–8 real example questions in CEO language. Highest-converting section. Triggers self-insertion. Pull from real client questions; invented ones read too clean.
9. **What makes it different** — 3 differentiators (live data not generic model; knows your business; answers not dashboards). Handles the unspoken "how is this different from ChatGPT" without asking it aloud.
10. **Proof and evidence** — steel-forged narrative, one client story, one quote. Placed after differentiation because proof means more once they know what's being proven.
11. **Objection handling** — security, data handling, integration, time required. Addressing data security unprompted is critical: you're asking for access to every email, call and transaction.
12. **Vision layer** — Company Brain → Company Hands → Digital Workforce → beyond. Placed deep intentionally: read early it's hype, read late it's trajectory. Also the section investors scroll for. One page, two audiences, resolved by sequence.
13. **Who this is for** — includes who it's NOT for. Status move that raises perceived value; operationally deflects poor-fit calls, protecting the 30-onboarding constraint.
14. **Final CTA** — restated claim, button, plus a line describing what the call actually is (scoping, not pitch) to remove sales-trap anxiety.
15. **Footer** — entity/GFC relationship, contact, legal, privacy. Trust signal checked more often than founders assume, especially by high-value buyers.

**Structural rules:** CTA appears minimum 3x (nav, after "what you can ask it" peak-desire moment, final). Never a CTA before the problem section. Whole page reads in under 3 minutes; sections are skimmable checkpoints that land in the headline alone.

## Visual/design direction

**Key correction issued:** Consumer luxury (gold, ornament, gloss) and enterprise luxury (restraint, space, precision) are opposite aesthetics. If the site looks like a watch brand, a CEO reads consultancy, not infrastructure. Target: quiet, dense, exact. Expensive because nothing is wasted. Reference set: Linear, Vercel, Stripe, Palantir.

**Color:** Near-black base (#0B0C0E / #101114), never pure black. Surface layers #16181C, #1E2126 for elevation via background shift rather than borders. Text never pure white — #F2F3F5 primary, #9BA1A8 secondary, #6B7178 tertiary. Exactly one accent — either cold platinum (#C8CDD4) or low-frequency ember (#C8642A). Accent appears on under 5% of the page; scarcity is the signal.

**Typography:** Two families max. Display: high-contrast neo-grotesque or restrained transitional serif (Söhne, Neue Haas Grotesk, GT America, or Editorial New/Signifier for a serif move — serif headlines signal institution and permanence). Body: Inter, Söhne, or Suisse International. Headline tracking -0.02 to -0.03em. Body line-height 1.6–1.7, size 17–19px. Max three weights sitewide. Tabular figures wherever numbers appear.

**Space:** Section padding 120–180px desktop. Content capped ~1100–1200px, text columns 65–70 characters. Asymmetry over centring. Empty space is the luxury signal.

**Motion:** Weight, not animation. Easing cubic-bezier(0.16, 1, 0.3, 1). 400–700ms entrances, 150–250ms hover feedback. Scroll reveals fade + 12–20px translate only, 60–80ms sibling stagger, trigger at ~15% into viewport. No scale-on-hover — shift elevation layer or accent opacity instead. Only ~1/3 of sections animate at all; static sections create rhythm. Respect prefers-reduced-motion.

**Graphics:** No stock photography under any circumstances. Three viable directions: (1) product as hero — real interface, real queries, dark surfaces, cropped at section edges [recommended primary]; (2) data as abstraction — 1px line work in tertiary grey, specific to the actual architecture, never generic node-and-line clichés [recommended as connective tissue]; (3) real industrial photography, only if genuine client-facility access exists [skip for launch].

**Texture:** 2–4% noise overlay on base background. Soft colourless radial elevation glow behind hero. Borders as rgba white at 6–8%, not solid grey. Corner radius small and consistent, 6–8px.

**Explicitly forbidden:** gold/metallic gradients/shine; purple-blue AI gradient; glassmorphism; stock photography; bounce/elastic easing; custom cursors; scale-on-hover; more than one accent; pure black or white; centred-everything layout.

**Review test:** could anything be removed without losing meaning? If yes, remove it.

## Naming — status

**Krinox rejected by Raj.** His own stated reason: it isn't sticking, he keeps forgetting it himself. Treated as a fatal signal. (krinox.com purchase previously planned for 27 July; superseded. Prior vault page "Podar Naming Decision — Krinox" is now obsolete.)

### Round 1 — REJECTED
Brief: strike a thought; open to famous fictional characters; "The Watcher" (Uatu) directionally right but boring; open to two-letter names; excluded Cortex/Pulse/Synapse types.

Reframe offered: The Watcher is boring because Uatu's defining trait is non-intervention. The product does the opposite — sees everything, then tells you. Target archetype is a narrator, not a watcher.

Candidates (all rejected as "not memorable"): Sanjaya (top rec — Mahabharata, divine sight narrating the battlefield to the blind king), Vidura, Chitragupta, Vyasa, Mimir, Muninn, Kvasir, Seldon, Varys, Mycroft, Demerzel, plus MU / NB / KV.

### Round 2 — REJECTED
Brief: SHORT names only. Cited Odin, Zeus, Sage as target register.

Fatal conflicts flagged in Raj's own examples:
- **Sage — DEAD.** Sage Group is a multi-billion-pound accounting/ERP software company. Naming an ERP-adjacent AI product after the category incumbent is fatal.
- **Zeus — compromised.** One of the most famous banking trojans in history. Wrong association for a product requesting access to every email and transaction.
- **Odin — survives but crowded.** Concept fits (traded an eye for wisdom; two ravens report back daily). Heavily used in tech/security, but not fatal.

Candidates (all rejected as "not catchy"): Norn, Metis, Scry, Maat, Rune, Thoth, Ken, Vigil, Nabu.

### Round 3 — current
Brief: famous and mainstream, yet out of the box. Raj likes Odin.

**Key strategic point raised:** Odin has survived three rounds in Raj's head; nothing else has. For a founder-led motion selling 30 deals in 90 days, memorability beats uniqueness. He is not fighting for SEO against other Odins — he is saying a name out loud to a CEO on a call. Crowding is a real problem at Series B, near-irrelevant at deal four. Recommendation: if Odin is what stuck, take Odin and stop spending cycles.

Candidates presented:
- **Horus** — the all-seeing eye. One of the most recognizable symbols on earth, draws itself as a logo, untaken in enterprise AI. Strongest answer to "famous but out of the box."
- **Merlin** — the advisor behind the throne. The entire Western archetype of counsel to a leader, zero explanation needed.
- **Argo** — the ship that carried the crew after the thing worth having. Famous through myth and a Best Picture winner. Four letters, hard consonants.
- **Neo** — the one who finally sees the code running underneath the world. Conceptually near-perfect; crowded in tech.
- **Raven** — Odin's ravens fly the world daily and report back. Keeps the Norse register, but is plain English.
- **Juno** — Roman goddess whose title Moneta means "the one who warns"; gave us both money and monitor. Mainstream via film and NASA mission.
- **Orion** — the hunter who crosses the entire sky. Instantly known, easy to say and draw.
- **Vulcan** — forge god plus the race defined by pure logic. Forge + logic is a strong double meaning for something built inside steel.
- **Mentat** — Dune's human computers who ingest all information and advise the ruler. Mainstream after two blockbusters; job description matches the product exactly.

Top three offered: Horus (symbol), Merlin (archetype), Argo (short and hard).

**Test proposed to Raj:** say Odin, Horus, Merlin and Argo out loud in a sentence describing the product. Whichever he can recall the next morning without checking the chat is the name.
