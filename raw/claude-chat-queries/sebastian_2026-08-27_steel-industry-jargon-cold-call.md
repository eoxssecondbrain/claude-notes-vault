Steel industry jargon for cold calls:

Product/mill terms
- HRC (Hot Rolled Coil) — steel coil rolled at high temp, less precise finish, cheaper. Base input for a lot of downstream products.
- CRC (Cold Rolled Coil) — HRC re-rolled cold for tighter tolerance and smoother finish. Costs more.
- HDG / GI (Hot Dip Galvanized) — steel coated in zinc for corrosion resistance.
- Coil vs. Sheet vs. Plate — coil is rolled continuous stock; sheet is cut flat pieces (thinner); plate is thicker cut flat stock (usually 3/16" and up).
- Gauge — thickness measurement; lower gauge number = thicker steel (opposite of what people expect).
- Grade — the chemical/mechanical spec of the steel (e.g., A36, A992) — determines strength and use.
- Heat number / Heat lot — the batch ID tracing steel back to the specific melt; used for quality traceability and mill certs.
- Mill cert (MTR – Mill Test Report) — document certifying chemical composition and mechanical properties of a specific heat.
- Tolerance — allowed variance from nominal thickness/width; tighter tolerance = higher precision, higher price.
- Camber / Crown / Edge wave — types of flatness defects in coil or sheet.
- Pickling — acid-cleaning process to remove mill scale from hot rolled steel before cold rolling or coating.
- Slitting — cutting wide coil into narrower widths.
- Coil ID / OD — inner diameter / outer diameter of a coil, relevant for mandrel/equipment compatibility.

Market/pricing terms
- Base price + extras — mill pricing structure; extras are add-ons for grade, gauge, width, tolerance, etc. on top of a base number.
- Index pricing (CRU, Platts, AMM) — third-party published steel price indices buyers/sellers reference instead of negotiating from scratch.
- Spot vs. Contract — one-off open-market purchase vs. a negotiated recurring supply agreement.
- Lead time — time from order to delivery; a major pain point in steel, often 6-12+ weeks from mills.
- Mill direct vs. Service center — buying straight from a producing mill (large minimums, long lead times) vs. a distributor/processor that stocks and cuts to size (faster, smaller orders).
- Toll processing — a service center processes material the customer already owns (cutting, slitting, coating) rather than selling their own inventory.
- Import duty / Section 232 tariffs — U.S. tariffs on imported steel; a recurring pricing and sourcing topic.
- Scrap surcharge — a price adjustment tied to the cost of scrap steel, common in mill pricing formulas.

Operations/supply chain terms
- MRP / ERP — Material Requirements Planning / Enterprise Resource Planning; the software systems that manage inventory, orders, and production (relevant since this is your own product category).
- Backorder — order that can't ship immediately because the specific item isn't in stock.
- Buy/sell — inventory a distributor already owns and resells, as opposed to broker/toll deals.
- Just-in-time (JIT) — inventory strategy minimizing on-hand stock, ordering close to when material is needed.
- Yield — the usable percentage of material after cutting/processing, minus scrap loss.

The two most useful for sounding credible fast: get HRC/CRC/gauge/grade right (product literacy), and know the difference between mill direct and service center plus what lead time and index pricing mean (supply chain literacy) — those come up in almost every steel conversation.
