Section 1 — Scoring architecture

Timeline first, because it is time-critical

Registration for Officer Scale I is open 1 September to 21 September 2026, with 4,256 vacancies (up from 3,928). Prelims is 21–22 November 2026 and Mains is 20 December 2026. That is 19 days to apply, ~11 weeks to Prelims, ~15.5 weeks to Mains.

The Mains pattern

| Section | Q | Marks | Marks/Q |
|---|---|---|---|
| Reasoning | 40 | 50 | 1.25 |
| Quant | 40 | 50 | 1.25 |
| General Awareness | 40 | 40 | 1.00 |
| English | 40 | 40 | 1.00 |
| Computer Knowledge | 40 | 20 | 0.50 |
| Total | 200 | 200 | — |

120 minutes, composite timing across all five sections, with English or Hindi as the language choice. Composite is the single most important structural fact: unlike Prelims, nobody forces you out of a section. Your time allocation is entirely your decision, and it is where most candidates lose 15–20 marks before answering a single question.

Marking math that changes behaviour

One-fourth of the marks are deducted for each wrong answer. Note a source conflict: some sources describe it as a flat 0.25 per wrong answer, which would differ on 1.25-mark questions. IBPS's own wording is 1/4 of the marks assigned to that question, so assume 0.3125 on Reasoning/Quant. This does not change strategy.

Guessing rule derived from that penalty: with 5 options, a blind guess is exactly EV-neutral (0.2 × 1 − 0.8 × 0.25 = 0). Eliminate two options and your accuracy goes to ~33%, making the guess clearly positive. So the rule is not "never guess" — it is never guess blind, always guess after eliminating two.

Marks per minute — the real leverage ranking

| Section | Marks | Realistic time | Marks/min |
|---|---|---|---|
| GA | 40 | 8–10 min | ~4.5 |
| Computer | 20 | 10–12 min | ~1.8 |
| English | 40 | 25 min | ~1.6 |
| Reasoning | 50 | 35 min | ~1.4 |
| Quant | 50 | 35 min | ~1.4 |

GA is roughly three times more time-efficient than anything else in the paper, and it is the only section whose outcome is fully decided before you enter the hall. Computer Knowledge is the second-most efficient despite carrying the lowest marks per question — 20 marks in ~11 minutes. Most aspirants under-prepare exactly these two because they look "small," and over-invest in Quant, which is the lowest-yield section per hour of prep at this difficulty level.

The score you are actually chasing

Two different thresholds, and they get confused constantly.

Threshold 1 — qualify for interview. You must clear both a sectional minimum in all five sections and the state-wise, category-wise overall cutoff; marks are normalised across shifts before the cutoff is applied. 2025 Mains overall cutoffs, UR, out of 200: Himachal Pradesh 84.06, Haryana 84.88, Bihar 73.81, Gujarat 59.63, Assam 61.38, Chhattisgarh 66.56, Jharkhand 73.69.

Threshold 2 — actual selection. Prelims does not count at all; final merit is Mains and Interview in an 80:20 ratio, with the interview out of 100 and a 40% minimum (35% for SC/ST/OBC/PwBD). The highest final cutoff in the 2025 cycle was 55.08 out of 100, for Tamil Nadu UR — single-source, treat as indicative.

Working that backwards: a final score of 55 with a 60/100 interview (12/20) requires 43/80 from Mains, i.e. 107.5/200. With a weak 50/100 interview, it requires ~112/200.

Target band: 110–120/200 in competitive states, 95–105 in softer ones. The qualifying cutoff of ~84 is a floor, not a goal — clearing it and stopping there gets you an interview call and no job.

The 25-mark decision you make before studying

The 2025 UR Mains cutoff spread runs from roughly 59.63 in Gujarat to 84.88 in Haryana. Choosing where you apply is worth about 25 marks — more than any single topic in this entire masterclass will give you. Constraint: RRB posts carry a local-language proficiency requirement verified at document verification, so this is not a free choice. But it is a real lever, and it locks permanently on 21 September. If the application is not yet submitted, that decision outranks every study action for the next 19 days.
