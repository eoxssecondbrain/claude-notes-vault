Section 1 — Scoring architecture

Timeline first, because it is time-critical

Registration for Officer Scale I is open 1 September to 21 September 2026, with 4,256 vacancies (up from 3,928). Prelims is 21–22 November 2026 and Mains is 20 December 2026. That is 19 days to apply, ~11 weeks to Prelims, ~15.5 weeks to Mains.

The Mains pattern

| Section | Q | Marks | Marks/Q |
|---|---|---|---|
| Reasoning | 40 | 50 | 1.25 |
| Quant | 40 | 50 | 1.25 |
| General Awareness | 40 | 40 | 1.00 |
| English | 40 | 40 | 1.00 |
| Computer Knowledge | 40 | 20 | 0.50 |
| Total | 200 | 200 | — |

120 minutes, composite timing across all five sections, with English or Hindi as the language choice. Composite is the single most important structural fact: unlike Prelims, nobody forces you out of a section. Your time allocation is entirely your decision, and it is where most candidates lose 15–20 marks before answering a single question.

Marking math that changes behaviour

One-fourth of the marks are deducted for each wrong answer. Note a source conflict: some sources describe it as a flat 0.25 per wrong answer, which would differ on 1.25-mark questions. IBPS's own wording is 1/4 of the marks assigned to that question, so assume 0.3125 on Reasoning/Quant. This does not change strategy.

Guessing rule derived from that penalty: with 5 options, a blind guess is exactly EV-neutral (0.2 × 1 − 0.8 × 0.25 = 0). Eliminate two options and your accuracy goes to ~33%, making the guess clearly positive. So the rule is not "never guess" — it is never guess blind, always guess after eliminating two.

Marks per minute — the real leverage ranking

| Section | Marks | Realistic time | Marks/min |
|---|---|---|---|

| Computer | 20 | 10–12 min | ~1.8 |

GA is roughly three times more time-efficient than anything else in the paper, and it is the only section whose outcome is fully decided before you enter the hall. Computer Knowledge is the second-most efficient despite carrying the lowest marks per question — 20 marks in ~11 minutes. Most aspirants under-prepare exactly these two because they look "small," and over-invest in Quant, which is the lowest-yield section per hour of prep at this difficulty level.

The score you are actually chasing

Two different thresholds, and they get confused constantly.

Threshold 1 — qualify for interview. You must clear both a sectional minimum in all five sections and the state-wise, category-wise overall cutoff; marks are normalised across shifts before the cutoff is applied. 2025 Mains overall cutoffs, UR, out of 200: Himachal Pradesh 84.06, Haryana 84.88, Bihar 73.81, Gujarat 59.63, Assam 61.38, Chhattisgarh 66.56, Jharkhand 73.69.

Threshold 2 — actual selection. Prelims does not count at all; final merit is Mains and Interview in an 80:20 ratio, with the interview out of 100 and a 40% minimum (35% for SC/ST/OBC/PwBD). The highest final cutoff in the 2025 cycle was 55.08 out of 100, for Tamil Nadu UR — single-source, treat as indicative.

Working that backwards: a final score of 55 with a 60/100 interview (12/20) requires 43/80 from Mains, i.e. 107.5/200. With a weak 50/100 interview, it requires ~112/200.

Target band: 110–120/200 in competitive states, 95–105 in softer ones. The qualifying cutoff of ~84 is a floor, not a goal — clearing it and stopping there gets you an interview call and no job.

The 25-mark decision you make before studying

The 2025 UR Mains cutoff spread runs from roughly 59.63 in Gujarat to 84.88 in Haryana. Choosing where you apply is worth about 25 marks — more than any single topic in this entire masterclass will give you. Constraint: RRB posts carry a local-language proficiency requirement verified at document verification, so this is not a free choice. But it is a real lever, and it locks permanently on 21 September. If the application is not yet submitted, that decision outranks every study action for the next 19 days.

2. Core concepts and shortcuts

Puzzles
- Classify before solving. Fixed-frame (floors, boxes, months, years — slots predetermined) closes faster than floating-frame (linear, circular, random with double entity). Do fixed-frame first, always.
- Clue order: definite placements → adjacency/immediate relations → gap-based ("two persons between") → negative clues last. Most people burn time processing negatives early, which fix nothing.
- Double-entity puzzles: build the skeleton using the variable with more definite clues; the second attribute resolves only after the skeleton locks. Never track both simultaneously.
- Two-branch abort rule: if three definite clues have been used and you still have more than two live cases, leave it. Beyond two branches at Mains difficulty, expected time exceeds expected marks.

Logical / Critical Reasoning
- Inference means must-be-true from the passage alone. No outside knowledge, no plausible-sounding extensions.
- Strengthen = supply a supporting premise or kill the alternative cause. Weaken = introduce an alternative cause or break the causal link.
- Assumption: use the negation test. Negate the option; if the argument collapses, that is the assumption.
- Two standard trap options: true but irrelevant, and correct in direction but overstated (all, never, only, must).

Machine Input–Output
- Identify the operation class first: positional shifting, arithmetic on numbers, or word + operator combined.
- Compare input to Step 1, and last step to second-last. You almost never need the full chain.
- Recurring logics: leftmost/rightmost pick-up, alphabetical or reverse-alphabetical arrangement, ascending/descending numeric, digit-sum or digit-product transformation.
- If the question asks only for Step 3, stop at Step 3.

Syllogism
- Only a few A are B ⇒ Some A are B (true) and Some A are not B (true); All A are B is false.
- All A are B ⇒ Some B are A always follows.
- Two negatives, or two particulars, give no conclusion.
- Possibility conclusions hold unless definitely contradicted. Skip Venn diagrams unless the question asks about possibility.

Inequality
- A conclusion follows only if an unbroken same-direction chain connects the two terms. Any reversal or any = break kills it.
- Either–or applies only when both conclusions involve the same pair and are complementary, and neither is individually true.
- Coded inequality: convert every symbol to a standard sign before reading the statement.

Data Sufficiency
- Never solve. Only test whether a unique answer is reachable.
- Order: statement I alone → statement II alone → combined. Stop at the first point sufficiency is established.
- The single most common Mains error is fully solving a DS puzzle. That is a 3-minute investment for 1.25 marks.

Blood Relation and Ranking
- Symbolise immediately: + male, − female, = spouse, vertical line for generation. Read the question before the paragraph and extract only the connecting chain.
- Total persons = position from left + position from right − 1.

3. Golden rules of attempting

Rule 1 — bank the standalone sets first, in 12 minutes. Machine Input–Output (5), Blood Relation (3), Syllogism/Inequality (3–4), miscellaneous word/number (1–2), plus one fixed-frame puzzle (5). That is roughly 18 questions and 22 marks at high accuracy before you touch a hard puzzle.

Rule 2 — take Critical Reasoning before the second puzzle. It requires no diagram, no case-splitting, and runs ~45 seconds per question. It is the cheapest remaining block, and most candidates leave it because it feels unfamiliar.

Rule 3 — hard-abort puzzles at 4 minutes, maximum two attempted. A failed puzzle costs 6.25 marks of opportunity plus the time. And never guess across an unsolved puzzle's five questions: five wrong answers is −1.56, wiping out a correct set elsewhere.

Time budget: 33–35 minutes. Target 26–30 attempts at 85%+ accuracy, giving 30–34 of 50.

Strategic caveat you should not ignore

Reasoning consistently shows the highest good-attempt figures in the paper — 24–30 in 2024, 23–27 in 2025. High attempt rates mean everyone scores here, so marginal advantage is thin. The differentiator at 110+ is not solving one more puzzle; it is accuracy plus the sections nobody prepares. If you have one spare hour, GA returns more than Reasoning does.

2. Core concepts and shortcuts

Non-negotiable memory base
- Fraction-percentage equivalents through 1/20 (1/8 = 12.5%, 1/16 = 6.25%, 1/12 = 8.33%). DI is percentage manipulation; long division is what kills the section.
- Squares to 30, cubes to 20, tables to 25.

Formulas that recur every year
- Successive percentage change: net = a + b + ab/100.
- Same SP, +x% and −x%: always a loss of x²/100 percent.
- CI − SI, 2 years = P(r/100)². For 3 years = P(r/100)²(3 + r/100).
- Time & Work: set total work = LCM of the given days, then efficiency = total ÷ days. Never work in fractions.
- Efficiency ratio is the inverse of time ratio. Outlet pipes carry negative efficiency.
- Equal distances: average speed = 2ab/(a+b). Same distance ⇒ speed ratio is the inverse of time ratio.
- Boats: downstream = b+s, upstream = b−s; boat = (d+u)/2, stream = (d−u)/2.
- Partnership profit ratio = capital × time.
- Replacement/mixture: final quantity = initial × (1 − x/V)ⁿ. Two-component averages: alligation cross method.
- Averages: use deviation from an assumed mean, not raw summation.

DI method
- Read the question stem before reading the data. Extract only the cells you need; never build the whole table.
- Approximate by default. Compute exactly only when two options sit within ~5% of each other.
- Caselet: spend the first 45 seconds converting prose into a table. Re-reading a caselet mid-solve is the biggest single time leak in this section.
- Missing-table DI: locate the one cell the question depends on and solve backwards to it.

Number series
- Test in fixed order: constant difference → second-level difference → multiplicative (×2±1, ×1.5, etc.) → squares/cubes ± constant → prime or alternating pattern.
- Wrong-number series: validate the pattern on three consecutive terms before checking the rest.

Data Sufficiency and Quantity Comparison
- Same discipline as reasoning DS: establish solvability, do not solve. Statement I, then II, then combined, stopping at first sufficiency.

3. Golden rules of attempting

Rule 1 — triage the DI sets before solving any of them. Spend 60 seconds scanning all four sets and rank them: table and bar (direct reads) first, pie second, caselet last. Solving sets in printed order is the default mistake and costs 5–8 marks.

Rule 2 — bank the standalone block in the first 12 minutes. Number series (4–6), one easy DI set (5), and 4–5 single-line arithmetic problems. That is 14–16 questions at high accuracy before you meet anything hard.

Rule 3 — hard-abort any DI set where you cannot answer three of five questions within 3.5 minutes, and drop it entirely rather than guessing across it. Also skip standalone mensuration, probability, and permutation questions when they appear as singles; they are 1–2 questions with disproportionate time cost.

Time budget: 33–35 minutes. Target 20–24 attempts at ~85% accuracy, giving 22–27 of 50.

Strategic caveat

Quant is the widest scoring spread in the paper and simultaneously the worst return per hour of preparation. For a 110–120 target you need roughly 22–27 marks here, not 40. The correct goal is DI speed and arithmetic accuracy on a fixed shortlist of topics, not comprehensive Quant mastery. Candidates who invert this — grinding Quant while leaving GA and Computer half-prepared — are the ones who land at 85–95 and miss merit despite clearing the qualifying cutoff.
