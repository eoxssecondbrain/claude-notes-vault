Section 1 — Scoring architecture

Timeline first, because it is time-critical

Registration for Officer Scale I is open 1 September to 21 September 2026, with 4,256 vacancies (up from 3,928). Prelims is 21–22 November 2026 and Mains is 20 December 2026. That is 19 days to apply, ~11 weeks to Prelims, ~15.5 weeks to Mains.

The Mains pattern

| Section | Q | Marks | Marks/Q |
|---|---|---|---|
| Reasoning | 40 | 50 | 1.25 |
| Quant | 40 | 50 | 1.25 |
| General Awareness | 40 | 40 | 1.00 |
| English | 40 | 40 | 1.00 |
| Computer Knowledge | 40 | 20 | 0.50 |
| Total | 200 | 200 | — |

120 minutes, composite timing across all five sections, with English or Hindi as the language choice. Composite is the single most important structural fact: unlike Prelims, nobody forces you out of a section. Your time allocation is entirely your decision, and it is where most candidates lose 15–20 marks before answering a single question.

Marking math that changes behaviour

One-fourth of the marks are deducted for each wrong answer. Note a source conflict: some sources describe it as a flat 0.25 per wrong answer, which would differ on 1.25-mark questions. IBPS's own wording is 1/4 of the marks assigned to that question, so assume 0.3125 on Reasoning/Quant. This does not change strategy.

Guessing rule derived from that penalty: with 5 options, a blind guess is exactly EV-neutral (0.2 × 1 − 0.8 × 0.25 = 0). Eliminate two options and your accuracy goes to ~33%, making the guess clearly positive. So the rule is not "never guess" — it is never guess blind, always guess after eliminating two.

Computer Knowledge is the second-most efficient despite carrying the lowest marks per question — 20 marks in ~11 minutes. Most aspirants under-prepare exactly these two because they look "small," and over-invest in Quant, which is the lowest-yield section per hour of prep at this difficulty level.

The score you are actually chasing

Two different thresholds, and they get confused constantly.

Threshold 1 — qualify for interview. You must clear both a sectional minimum in all five sections and the state-wise, category-wise overall cutoff; marks are normalised across shifts before the cutoff is applied. 2025 Mains overall cutoffs, UR, out of 200: Himachal Pradesh 84.06, Haryana 84.88, Bihar 73.81, Gujarat 59.63, Assam 61.38, Chhattisgarh 66.56, Jharkhand 73.69.

Threshold 2 — actual selection. Prelims does not count at all; final merit is Mains and Interview in an 80:20 ratio, with the interview out of 100 and a 40% minimum (35% for SC/ST/OBC/PwBD). The highest final cutoff in the 2025 cycle was 55.08 out of 100, for Tamil Nadu UR — single-source, treat as indicative.

Working that backwards: a final score of 55 with a 60/100 interview (12/20) requires 43/80 from Mains, i.e. 107.5/200. With a weak 50/100 interview, it requires ~112/200.

Target band: 110–120/200 in competitive states, 95–105 in softer ones. The qualifying cutoff of ~84 is a floor, not a goal — clearing it and stopping there gets you an interview call and no job.

The 25-mark decision you make before studying

The 2025 UR Mains cutoff spread runs from roughly 59.63 in Gujarat to 84.88 in Haryana. Choosing where you apply is worth about 25 marks — more than any single topic in this entire masterclass will give you. Constraint: RRB posts carry a local-language proficiency requirement verified at document verification, so this is not a free choice. But it is a real lever, and it locks permanently on 21 September. If the application is not yet submitted, that decision outranks every study action for the next 19 days.

2. Core concepts and shortcuts

Puzzles
- Classify before solving. Fixed-frame (floors, boxes, months, years — slots predetermined) closes faster than floating-frame (linear, circular, random with double entity). Do fixed-frame first, always.
- Clue order: definite placements → adjacency/immediate relations → gap-based ("two persons between") → negative clues last. Most people burn time processing negatives early, which fix nothing.
- Double-entity puzzles: build the skeleton using the variable with more definite clues; the second attribute resolves only after the skeleton locks. Never track both simultaneously.
- Two-branch abort rule: if three definite clues have been used and you still have more than two live cases, leave it. Beyond two branches at Mains difficulty, expected time exceeds expected marks.

Logical / Critical Reasoning
- Inference means must-be-true from the passage alone. No outside knowledge, no plausible-sounding extensions.
- Strengthen = supply a supporting premise or kill the alternative cause. Weaken = introduce an alternative cause or break the causal link.
- Assumption: use the negation test. Negate the option; if the argument collapses, that is the assumption.
- Two standard trap options: true but irrelevant, and correct in direction but overstated (all, never, only, must).

Machine Input–Output
- Identify the operation class first: positional shifting, arithmetic on numbers, or word + operator combined.
- Compare input to Step 1, and last step to second-last. You almost never need the full chain.
- Recurring logics: leftmost/rightmost pick-up, alphabetical or reverse-alphabetical arrangement, ascending/descending numeric, digit-sum or digit-product transformation.
- If the question asks only for Step 3, stop at Step 3.

Syllogism
- Only a few A are B ⇒ Some A are B (true) and Some A are not B (true); All A are B is false.
- All A are B ⇒ Some B are A always follows.
- Two negatives, or two particulars, give no conclusion.
- Possibility conclusions hold unless definitely contradicted. Skip Venn diagrams unless the question asks about possibility.

Inequality
- A conclusion follows only if an unbroken same-direction chain connects the two terms. Any reversal or any = break kills it.
- Either–or applies only when both conclusions involve the same pair and are complementary, and neither is individually true.
- Coded inequality: convert every symbol to a standard sign before reading the statement.

Data Sufficiency
- Never solve. Only test whether a unique answer is reachable.
- Order: statement I alone → statement II alone → combined. Stop at the first point sufficiency is established.
- The single most common Mains error is fully solving a DS puzzle. That is a 3-minute investment for 1.25 marks.

Blood Relation and Ranking
- Symbolise immediately: + male, − female, = spouse, vertical line for generation. Read the question before the paragraph and extract only the connecting chain.
- Total persons = position from left + position from right − 1.

3. Golden rules of attempting

Rule 1 — bank the standalone sets first, in 12 minutes. Machine Input–Output (5), Blood Relation (3), Syllogism/Inequality (3–4), miscellaneous word/number (1–2), plus one fixed-frame puzzle (5). That is roughly 18 questions and 22 marks at high accuracy before you touch a hard puzzle.

Rule 2 — take Critical Reasoning before the second puzzle. It requires no diagram, no case-splitting, and runs ~45 seconds per question. It is the cheapest remaining block, and most candidates leave it because it feels unfamiliar.

Rule 3 — hard-abort puzzles at 4 minutes, maximum two attempted. A failed puzzle costs 6.25 marks of opportunity plus the time. And never guess across an unsolved puzzle's five questions: five wrong answers is −1.56, wiping out a correct set elsewhere.

Time budget: 33–35 minutes. Target 26–30 attempts at 85%+ accuracy, giving 30–34 of 50.

Strategic caveat you should not ignore

Reasoning consistently shows the highest good-attempt figures in the paper — 24–30 in 2024, 23–27 in 2025. High attempt rates mean everyone scores here, so marginal advantage is thin. The differentiator at 110+ is not solving one more puzzle; it is accuracy plus the sections nobody prepares. If you have one spare hour, GA returns more than Reasoning does.

2. Core concepts and shortcuts

Non-negotiable memory base
- Fraction-percentage equivalents through 1/20 (1/8 = 12.5%, 1/16 = 6.25%, 1/12 = 8.33%). DI is percentage manipulation; long division is what kills the section.
- Squares to 30, cubes to 20, tables to 25.

Formulas that recur every year
- Successive percentage change: net = a + b + ab/100.
- Same SP, +x% and −x%: always a loss of x²/100 percent.
- CI − SI, 2 years = P(r/100)². For 3 years = P(r/100)²(3 + r/100).
- Time & Work: set total work = LCM of the given days, then efficiency = total ÷ days. Never work in fractions.
- Efficiency ratio is the inverse of time ratio. Outlet pipes carry negative efficiency.
- Equal distances: average speed = 2ab/(a+b). Same distance ⇒ speed ratio is the inverse of time ratio.
- Boats: downstream = b+s, upstream = b−s; boat = (d+u)/2, stream = (d−u)/2.
- Partnership profit ratio = capital × time.
- Replacement/mixture: final quantity = initial × (1 − x/V)ⁿ. Two-component averages: alligation cross method.
- Averages: use deviation from an assumed mean, not raw summation.

DI method
- Read the question stem before reading the data. Extract only the cells you need; never build the whole table.
- Approximate by default. Compute exactly only when two options sit within ~5% of each other.
- Caselet: spend the first 45 seconds converting prose into a table. Re-reading a caselet mid-solve is the biggest single time leak in this section.
- Missing-table DI: locate the one cell the question depends on and solve backwards to it.

Number series
- Test in fixed order: constant difference → second-level difference → multiplicative (×2±1, ×1.5, etc.) → squares/cubes ± constant → prime or alternating pattern.
- Wrong-number series: validate the pattern on three consecutive terms before checking the rest.

Data Sufficiency and Quantity Comparison
- Same discipline as reasoning DS: establish solvability, do not solve. Statement I, then II, then combined, stopping at first sufficiency.

3. Golden rules of attempting

Rule 1 — triage the DI sets before solving any of them. Spend 60 seconds scanning all four sets and rank them: table and bar (direct reads) first, pie second, caselet last. Solving sets in printed order is the default mistake and costs 5–8 marks.

Rule 2 — bank the standalone block in the first 12 minutes. Number series (4–6), one easy DI set (5), and 4–5 single-line arithmetic problems. That is 14–16 questions at high accuracy before you meet anything hard.

Rule 3 — hard-abort any DI set where you cannot answer three of five questions within 3.5 minutes, and drop it entirely rather than guessing across it. Also skip standalone mensuration, probability, and permutation questions when they appear as singles; they are 1–2 questions with disproportionate time cost.

Time budget: 33–35 minutes. Target 20–24 attempts at ~85% accuracy, giving 22–27 of 50.

Strategic caveat

Quant is the widest scoring spread in the paper and simultaneously the worst return per hour of preparation. For a 110–120 target you need roughly 22–27 marks here, not 40. The correct goal is DI speed and arithmetic accuracy on a fixed shortlist of topics, not comprehensive Quant mastery. Candidates who invert this — grinding Quant while leaving GA and Computer half-prepared — are the ones who land at 85–95 and miss merit despite clearing the qualifying cutoff.

2. Core concepts and content map

RRB-specific banking (routinely asked, routinely skipped)
- RRB structure: established under the RRB Act 1976; ownership split 50% Central Government, 35% sponsor bank, 15% State Government. Amalgamation waves and the current count of RRBs and their sponsor banks.
- NABARD's role: refinance, supervision of RRBs and cooperatives, SHG-bank linkage, KCC, RIDF.
- Priority sector lending target for RRBs: 75% of ANBC, with sub-targets for agriculture, small and marginal farmers, micro enterprises, and weaker sections.

RBI and monetary policy
- Repo, SDF (replaced reverse repo as the floor), MSF, bank rate, CRR, SLR — current values and the direction of the last three MPC decisions.
- MPC: six members, three external, Governor holds the casting vote; meets bi-monthly.
- Basel III in India: CRAR 9%, capital conservation buffer 2.5%, plus LCR and NSFR.

Banking terms that recur
- NPA classification: SMA-0/1/2 → substandard → doubtful → loss.
- DICGC deposit insurance cover of ₹5 lakh.
- CASA, NIM, CD ratio, provisioning coverage ratio.
- Payment rails: UPI, IMPS, NEFT, RTGS, NACH, Bharat BillPay, CBDC e-rupee, UPI internationalisation.

Schemes (highest current-affairs overlap)
- PMJDY, PMJJBY, PMSBY, APY — premium and cover amounts.
- MUDRA categories and limits, Stand-Up India, PM Vishwakarma, PMFBY, PM SVANidhi.

Current-affairs categories that repeat every year
Appointments, awards, defence exercises, summits and their host countries, government schemes and launches, GI tags, sports winners and venues, indices and rankings, MoUs, obituaries, books and authors.

Static, restricted to the profitable subset
Bank headquarters and taglines, national parks and wildlife sanctuaries, dams and their rivers, currencies and capitals, important days, RBI governors. Nothing beyond this list is worth time.

Method
- One current-affairs source only. A monthly PDF plus one consolidated capsule. Multi-sourcing GA creates volume without recall.
- Active recall, not reading. A daily 20-question quiz beats an hour of passive revision.
- Convert weekly to one-line facts; revise the one-liners, never the original articles.
- Final 30 days: capsules and one-liners only, no new intake beyond a weekly top-up.

3. Golden rules of attempting

Rule 1 — GA goes first in the paper, capped hard at 8–10 minutes, single pass, no revisit. It is the only section where marks are already decided; spending 15 minutes cannot manufacture knowledge you did not bring.

Rule 2 — 12-second rule per question. You either know it or you don't. Second-guessing in GA flips correct answers to incorrect far more often than the reverse.

Rule 3 — apply the elimination test before guessing. Attempt only if two options are gone; a blind guess is expected-value zero and only adds variance around your cutoff.

Target: 22–28 attempts at ~90% accuracy, giving 20–25 of 40.

The strategic point about this section

GA is the highest marks-per-minute block in the paper — 40 marks in nine minutes — and the only one whose outcome is fully sealed before you sit down. It is also the section most candidates postpone until after Prelims, which leaves 22 November to 20 December, four weeks, to cover a six-month window. That compression is the single most common reason competent candidates land at 85–95 instead of 110+.

Concrete implication: GA cannot be block-studied later. It needs a 20-minute daily habit starting now, running through both Prelims and Mains. Of everything in this masterclass, that habit has the highest expected return per hour invested — higher than any Quant topic.

2. Core concepts

Memory and hardware
- Hierarchy by speed: registers → cache → RAM → secondary storage. Shortest access time is registers, then cache.
- Volatile: RAM (SRAM in cache, DRAM as main memory). Non-volatile: ROM, HDD, SSD, flash, magnetic tape, optical disc.
- WORM = write once, read many (CD-R and similar optical media).
- CPU parts: ALU performs arithmetic and logical operations; the control unit directs and sequences data flow but performs no computation; registers hold operands.
- Units: bit → nibble (4) → byte (8) → KB → MB → GB → TB → PB → EB → ZB → YB.
- Generations: vacuum tubes → transistors → integrated circuits → microprocessors → AI.

Software
- System software: OS, compilers, assemblers, device drivers, utilities (anti-virus is a utility, and therefore system software). Application software is everything user-facing.
- Compiler translates the whole program at once and executes faster; interpreter goes line by line; assembler converts assembly to machine code.

Networking
- Scope: PAN → LAN → MAN → WAN. Bluetooth and Wireless USB are wireless PAN.
- Devices by layer: repeater and hub at layer 1, switch and bridge at layer 2, router at layer 3, gateway across layers, modem for modulation.
- Topologies: bus, star, ring, mesh, tree.
- OSI has seven layers; TCP/IP has four.
- Protocols and ports worth memorising: HTTP 80, HTTPS 443, FTP 20/21, SMTP 25 (sending mail), POP3 110, IMAP 143, DNS 53, Telnet 23, DHCP 67/68.
- IPv4 is 32-bit, IPv6 is 128-bit.

Security
- Virus attaches to a host file; worm self-replicates without a host; trojan disguises itself; ransomware encrypts and demands payment; spyware monitors covertly; keylogger records keystrokes; rootkit hides privileged access; botnet is a network of compromised machines.
- Social engineering variants: phishing (email or fake site), spear phishing (targeted), vishing (voice), smishing (SMS), pharming (DNS redirection).
- Defences: firewall, anti-virus, encryption (symmetric uses one key, asymmetric uses a key pair), digital signature, SSL/TLS, two-factor authentication.

MS Office
- Extensions: .docx, .xlsx, .pptx.
- Universal shortcuts: Ctrl + C/V/X/Z/Y, B/I/U, F/H, N/S/P; F7 for spell check.
- Excel specifics: F2 edits a cell, Alt + = inserts autosum, Ctrl + ; inserts the date, $ makes a reference absolute, freeze panes locks headers. COUNT counts numbers, COUNTA counts non-empty cells. VLOOKUP, IF, CONCATENATE.
- PowerPoint: F5 starts the slideshow from the beginning.

DBMS
- Command families: DDL (CREATE, ALTER, DROP, TRUNCATE), DML (SELECT, INSERT, UPDATE, DELETE), DCL (GRANT, REVOKE), TCL (COMMIT, ROLLBACK, SAVEPOINT).
- Keys: primary, foreign, candidate, unique, composite.
- Terminology: table = relation, row = tuple or record, column = attribute or field. Normal forms 1NF, 2NF, 3NF.

Operating systems
- Functions: process, memory, file, and device management.
- Types: batch, multiprogramming, time-sharing, real-time, distributed.
- Concepts: scheduling (FCFS, SJF, round robin), deadlock, virtual memory and paging.

Banking technology (overlaps GA, so it double-counts)
- NPCI stable: UPI, IMPS, RuPay, BHIM, AePS, NACH, FASTag. Also NEFT, RTGS, CTS, SWIFT, CBS, CBDC.
- IFSC is 11 characters, MICR is 9 digits.

3. Golden rules of attempting

Rule 1 — Computer goes second, immediately after GA, capped at 10–12 minutes. Both are pure-recall sections; front-loading them banks ~35 marks in about 20 minutes and leaves 100 minutes for the three solving sections.

Rule 2 — 20-second ceiling per question, no exceptions. If the term is unfamiliar, move on. Reasoning your way to a computer answer almost never works and always costs time.

Rule 3 — guess more aggressively here than anywhere else. A wrong answer costs 0.125, the cheapest penalty in the paper, against 0.3125 in Reasoning and Quant. Eliminating even one option makes a guess positive-value here.

Target: 28–34 attempts at ~90% accuracy, giving 13–16 of 20.

Why this section is mispriced

Twenty marks looks small, so candidates skip it. But the syllabus is finite, static, and has no year-to-year variance — roughly 15–20 hours of preparation caps out the section permanently. The same 15 marks in Quant costs an order of magnitude more time and still carries difficulty risk each year.

The correct framing is not that Computer wins you merit — it can't, it's capped at 20. It's that Computer plus GA together are 60 marks obtainable in about 20 minutes of exam time and a fixed, bounded prep investment. Locking those two down is what makes a 110+ total arithmetically possible without needing 40/50 in Quant.

2. Core concepts and shortcuts

Reading Comprehension
- RRB Mains passages are descriptive and fact-heavy (migration, personal finance, environment, aviation), not abstract. Typically 3 of 5 questions per set are direct retrieval.
- Do vocabulary questions (synonym/antonym in context) first — they need the sentence, not the passage.
- Question-first approach for detail questions: read the question, scan for the keyword, answer. Read fully only for theme and tone questions.
- Inference questions: eliminate any option containing extreme quantifiers (all, never, only) or outside knowledge.
- Never re-read a passage a third time. If a second scan fails to locate the answer, move on.

Grammar, restricted to the high-frequency zones
- Subject-verb agreement: "one of the + plural noun" takes a singular verb; either/or and neither/nor agree with the nearer subject; each and every are singular; collective nouns are singular unless members act individually.
- Preposition collocations that recur: comply with, prefer to, superior to, differ from (things) / differ with (people), capable of, accused of, insist on, deprived of, consists of.
- Tense: since/for with present perfect or present perfect continuous; past perfect for the earlier of two past events; conditionals — if + past + would, if + had + past participle + would have.
- Redundancy traps: return back, repeat again, reason is because, more better.
- Parallelism across list items and after correlative pairs (not only… but also).

Fillers
- Read the full sentence for direction markers before the blank: contrast (although, however, despite), continuation (moreover, furthermore), cause-effect (hence, consequently).
- For double fillers, solve the easier blank first and eliminate on that basis alone.

Word swap and replacement
- The two misplaced words are almost always the same part of speech. Test noun-with-noun and verb-with-verb pairs; do not try all combinations.

Jumbled sentences
- Find the opener: it stands alone, introduces the subject by full noun rather than pronoun, and contains no back-referencing connector.
- Chain via pronoun referents, connectors, and chronology. Fix mandatory pairs first, then eliminate options — do not construct the full sequence from scratch.

Idioms and phrases
- This is a memorisation block, not a skill block. A list of roughly 150 common idioms covers the recurring set, and it has been worth 3–6 questions in each recent paper. Cheapest marks in the section per hour spent.

Error detection
- Check in a fixed order every time: subject-verb agreement → tense → preposition → article → pronoun reference → parallelism. A fixed order prevents re-reading.

3. Golden rules of attempting

Rule 1 — standalones before passages. Idioms, fillers, word swap, error detection, and rearrangement together are 15–20 questions requiring no sustained reading. Bank them in 10 minutes, then move to RC.

Rule 2 — triage the RC sets. Read the first two lines of each passage, then rank them. Do the concrete, fact-based passage first; leave any abstract or opinion-heavy passage for last or skip it entirely.

Rule 3 — cap the section at 25 minutes and guess after eliminating two. English options frequently reduce to two credible choices, so the elimination bar for a positive-value guess is easier to clear here than anywhere except Computer.

Target: 26–30 attempts at 85–90% accuracy, giving 24–28 of 40.

Two things worth knowing about this section

First, it is the most reliable non-recall section in the paper. Quant spiked to moderate-difficult in 2023 with good attempts collapsing to 9–14; English has been rated easy to moderate every recent year with no comparable spike. A fixed six-week grammar-and-idiom effort produces a score that holds regardless of what the paper does.

Second, on your English-over-Hindi choice: published sectional qualifying scores from the 2023 Mains cycle show the English Language bar at 9.50 for General against 13.25 for Hindi Language, and 5.75 against 10.25 for reserved categories. The scale in that table is ambiguous (it lists a maximum of 40 for sections worth 50, suggesting normalised rather than raw marks), so read it as a relative signal, not an exact figure. The signal itself is clear: the English sectional bar sits materially lower than the Hindi one, which means the choice you have made is the easier one to clear. Same table also shows the GA sectional minimum at just 2.50 for General — confirming that GA is a merit lever, not a survival risk.

Section 7 — Integrated plan, risks, blind spots

A. The 120-minute attempt script

| Order | Section | Time | Target |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | General Awareness | 9 min | 22/40 |
| 2 | Computer Knowledge | 11 min | 14/20 |
| 3 | English | 25 min | 26/40 |
| 4 | Reasoning | 35 min | 31/50 |
| 5 | Quantitative Aptitude | 36 min | 24/50 |
| | Buffer | 4 min | — |
| | Total | 120 min | 117/200 |

Logic: the two pure-recall sections run first, locking 36 marks in 20 minutes with no cognitive load. English next because it is the most reliable of the three solving sections. Quant last, deliberately — it has the worst marginal return, so it should be the section that absorbs any time squeeze rather than the one that causes it.

The one legitimate variation: if your Quant accuracy collapses under fatigue, swap Reasoning and Quant. Test both orders across mocks in November and freeze one by 1 December. Do not decide this in the exam hall.

Note the arithmetic: GA, Computer, English and Reasoning alone total 93. Quant only needs to add 20–25. That is the whole point of the sequencing.

B. Week-by-week, 2 September to 20 December

Phase 0 — now to 21 September
Submit the application. Choose the state and RRB deliberately: the 2025 UR Mains cutoff spread ran from 59.63 in Gujarat to 84.88 in Haryana, constrained by the local-language proficiency requirement. This locks permanently on 21 September and is worth more than any topic in this document.

Phase 1 — 2 September to 20 November (11 weeks)
- Daily, non-negotiable: 20 minutes GA. One source, active recall, one-line notes. This runs all 15 weeks without a break.
- Daily: 30 minutes English grammar and the idiom list. Prelims has no English section, so this is pure Mains investment — and it is the only window in which it fits.
- Complete the entire Computer Knowledge syllabus by mid-October. It is 15–20 hours, it is finite, and it never needs revisiting. Doing it now costs slack time you will not have in December.
- Prelims core: Reasoning puzzles and Quant DI plus arithmetic speed, at Prelims difficulty.
- Mocks: 3 Prelims mocks per week to mid-October, 4–5 per week thereafter.

Phase 2 — 21 November to 20 December (4 weeks)
- Week 1: Mains-specific Reasoning (critical reasoning, machine input-output) and Caselet DI. Two full Mains mocks.
- Week 2: GA consolidation across July–November plus banking, RRB structure, and NABARD. Two mocks.
- Week 3: Three mocks with full analysis. English RC speed drills.
- Final 6 days: revision only — GA capsules, formula sheet, idiom list, computer notes. Maximum two mocks, none in the last 48 hours.

C. Mock discipline

This is where the actual differentiation happens, not in content.
- Analysis time must equal or exceed attempt time. A two-hour mock earns two hours of review.
- Keep an error log with four fields: question type, cause (concept gap, calculation slip, misreading, time overrun), the fix, and the date. Read it before every subsequent mock.
- Track marks per minute by section, not just total score. A section that returns under 1.0 marks per minute is being over-allocated.
- Freeze attempt order and time caps by 1 December. Strategy changes in the final fortnight reliably cost marks.

D. Risks and blind spots

1. The Prelims misallocation. Prelims marks are discarded entirely and shortlisting runs at roughly ten times vacancies. It deserves enough work to clear comfortably and not one hour more. It feels urgent because it comes first, which is exactly why people overspend on it.

2. The four-week gap is not a preparation window. Prelims on 21–22 November, Mains on 20 December, with results consuming part of that. GA, Computer, and English cannot be built in four weeks. Whatever exists by 20 November sets your ceiling.

3. Difficulty variance in Quant. In 2023 good attempts fell to 9–14. Build your target so that a bad Quant year still clears — which the 93-from-four-sections structure above does.

4. Conflicting information on sectional cutoffs. One source states there are no sectional cutoffs in Mains. Against that, the published 2023 cycle shows per-section qualifying scores, and another source describes clearing all five sectional minimums as mandatory alongside the state cutoff. Assume sectional minimums exist and never zero out a section. The cost of being wrong that way is nil; the cost of the reverse is disqualification.

5. Don't plan to the predicted cutoff. One source estimates the 2026 General Mains cutoff at 70–82 out of 200. Actual 2025 figures exceeded that in several states, including 84.06 in Himachal Pradesh and 84.88 in Haryana. Plan to the actuals, not the forecast.

6. The interview is not free. Roughly three times the vacancies are called, it carries 20% weight, and the highest 2025 final cutoff was 55.08 out of 100. The difference between a 50 and a 65 interview is worth about five marks of Mains. Budget two weeks for it in January rather than treating it as a formality.

E. What to ignore entirely

Quadratic equations and simplification for Mains. IBPS PO Mains English material. Static GK beyond the short list in Section 4. Coding-decoding at Mains level. Any new book, channel, or test series introduced after 1 December.

The three decisions that actually determine the outcome

State and RRB choice by 21 September. The GA habit starting today. Computer Knowledge finished before Prelims. Everything else in these seven sections is execution detail on top of those three.
