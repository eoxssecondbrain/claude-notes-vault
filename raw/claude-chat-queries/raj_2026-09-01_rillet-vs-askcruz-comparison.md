Rillet doubled new ARR in the last three months and now serves more than 600 customers, including publicly listed enterprises and the fastest growing AI companies of all time. Legacy systems were designed for humans to input and review data manually, while Rillet's platform was designed from the ground up for AI agents and humans to share the same real-time financial truth.

Where it's genuinely similar: the thesis. Both bets are "rebuild the system of record so agents and humans work inside the same data, not a chat layer bolted on top."

Where the analogy breaks — three places, checked against AskCruz's own documented architecture (OV2, self-knowledge doc v1.1, 21 Aug 2026):

1. What the system actually does. AskCruz's documented pipeline is Source Systems → Ingestion → Filtering → Sensitivity Classification → Central Knowledge Store → Knowledge Generation → Citation Check → Human Approval → Retrieval → Response. That's a retrieval-and-synthesis layer that answers questions with citations. IRIS, its query engine, is described in the vault as an AI conversational layer for real-time reporting/analytics on ERP data — a query tool, not a transactional agent. Rillet's agents write into the general ledger and execute finance work; a human approves the outcome of an action taken in the system of record. AskCruz's human-approval step, as documented, sits on the answer, not on a transaction. This is the actual distinction your own post draws between copilot and rebuild — worth sitting with, because on the current documented architecture AskCruz is closer to the copilot side of that line than the Rillet side.

2. Scope discipline. Rillet picked one function — accounting — that is structured, rule-governed, and must survive an audit, and rebuilt only that. Your own OV2 docs currently contain an unresolved contradiction: one AskCruz asset frames it as steel-vertical, another frames it explicitly as industry-agnostic, and a wiki flag notes these can't both be current positioning. Rillet's discipline was precisely refusing that kind of scope ambiguity.

3. Stage. Rillet: $200M+ raised, 600+ customers, 2 years public. AskCruz-side comparison would be 2 confirmed paying clients (Sabre Alloys, 3GM) at roughly $7K MRR, bootstrapped. Not a criticism of trajectory, just calibration — the LinkedIn post's "which function gets rebuilt next" question is the more useful takeaway than "are we doing what Rillet did."

The sharper opportunity read: if the Rillet playbook is the model — pick one structured, rule-governed, audit-bound workflow and rebuild it so agents act and humans approve — the better candidate inside your own stack isn't the Company Brain/knowledge layer at all. It's something transactional: order entry, quoting, ticket handling, MTR processing. Those are the pieces that are structured, rule-governed, and already sit inside a system of record. That reframes "what gets rebuilt next" as a real roadmap question rather than a positioning line.

ASSISTANT: You're partly right, but not for the reason you're framing it. The positioning difference is real, but it's not the main gap — the mechanism difference is.

Rillet's actual product does two things AskCruz doesn't yet, per its own documented architecture: agents write into the system of record (the general ledger), and the human approval step sits on the action taken, not on the answer given. AskCruz's documented pipeline ends at Citation Check → Human Approval → Retrieval → Response — the approval is on what gets said back to a user, not on a transaction executed inside Odoo. IRIS is a query layer, not a write layer. AI Workers is the closer candidate to "agent acts, human approves," but it isn't what AskCruz is being pitched or built around right now — Company Brain is.

So if you reposition AskCruz today as "Rillet for steel CFOs," you'd be selling a capability you haven't built yet. That's the actual risk in your read, not a minor one — a CFO buying Rillet is buying "the ledger runs itself and I approve outcomes." A CFO buying current AskCruz would be buying "I can ask my business data questions and get cited answers." Those are different purchases with different willingness to pay, and conflating them in positioning creates a sales-cycle mismatch: the pitch promises Rillet-grade autonomy, the product delivers a knowledge layer, and the gap surfaces in the first serious eval call.

Where you'd be right: if the plan is to reposition AskCruz's build roadmap toward CFO-facing transactional work — AI Workers doing structured, auditable actions (order entry, invoicing, quote approval) with a human sign-off on the action — then yes, that's the same play as Rillet, just applied to steel ops instead of accounting. That's a real, defensible version of your instinct. But it's a roadmap decision, not a positioning decision — you'd be committing engineering to build the write/approval layer, not just rewriting the pitch deck.
