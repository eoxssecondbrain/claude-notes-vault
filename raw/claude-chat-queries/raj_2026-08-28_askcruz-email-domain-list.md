Assistant: Two things before the list, since they affect whether this actually works:

**Deliverability risk with cheap TLDs.** For cold email sending domains, oddball TLDs (.xyz, .click, .top, .site, .online) get flagged or outright blocked by corporate email security filters — which is a real problem given your buyers are steel/metals distributors running conservative IT stacks. The whole point of a rotating domain pool is to look like normal business email, not to look cheap. Stick to .com, .net, .co, .org for anything actually sending mail.

**The <$10/year math.** True $10-and-under-forever pricing basically only exists as a first-year promo — renewals on almost every TLD jump (.xyz renews ~$11–13, .info ~$22, .online/.site often $20+ after year one). The one exception: at-cost registrars (Cloudflare Registrar, Porkbun) sell .com near wholesale, landing around $9–11/year with no markup, no upsells, indefinitely. That's the only way to get "boring, safe TLD" and "cheap" at the same time long-term. I'd register all 100 there rather than chasing $1 first-year deals on weird extensions.

A few practical notes:
- Register all 100 with the same registrar (Cloudflare or Porkbun) so renewal pricing stays predictable and you're not juggling logins.
- You don't need all 100 live simultaneously — most cold-email infra tools (Instantly, Smartlead, etc.) recommend 1 sending domain per 2–3 inboxes, so 100 domains supports a large multi-inbox setup; buy in this batch but activate/warm them in waves rather than all at once, or you'll trip spam filters from a sudden burst of new-domain activity.
- Some of these will already be registered — treat this as a shortlist to run through your registrar's bulk-availability checker, not a confirmed-available list.
