On the actual question — I checked askcruz.com directly. Your positioning isn't really "second brain" in the consumer-productivity sense (Notion, Mem, Tana). It's "Company Brain + Company Hands" for mid-size steel operations: ERP/email/calls unified, and the AI takes action (drafts quotes, flags at-risk orders). The closer comps are enterprise AI-ops and industrial-ops companies, not personal knowledge tools. Here's what those companies actually put on their homepages:

Glean ("Work AI" — unifies company knowledge across systems, closest conceptual match to "Company Brain"): clean animated explainer, shows siloed data becoming one searchable/answerable layer. Introducing Glean: Work AI for all — https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=b7qojk3Ju9U

Samsara (Connected Operations Cloud — sells an "operating system" to physical/industrial businesses, same buyer psychology as steel service centers): live-action plant/warehouse footage intercut with UI overlays, documentary tone, no cartoon abstraction. Samsara: The Connected Operations Cloud — https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=_2wYfEpe5X0, Make More Possible in Operations with Samsara — https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=sddu9-w49MI

Tettra (knowledge base for teams — smaller budget, more comparable to what you'd actually produce): short screen-capture demo with voiceover, shows the product answering a real question in seconds. Tettra Knowledge Base Explainer — https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=2UQsrdsw5r8

Thomson Reuters CoCounsel: customer-story format — a real user narrates the specific problem the AI solved, documentary style, no product-tour narration at all.

My take: don't copy Glean's abstract animation style — your buyer is a steel service center owner, not a SaaS-native knowledge worker, and abstraction reads as generic AI hype to that audience. The Samsara + CoCounsel combination fits your actual hero copy better, since you've already written the story: open on the real bottleneck ("the company already knows the answer, but only one person does, and they're on a truck, in a meeting, or gone") with live-action or realistic footage of a service center floor, then cut to a screen-capture of Cruz answering that exact question instantly and taking the action (drafting the quote, flagging the order). Close on "One brain. Every person on your floor." That's a 60–75 second video, cheaper than full animation, and it's credible to a steel-industry buyer because it shows their environment, not a stock SaaS dashboard fantasy.
