Review 1 — Creative director
- Strong open. The 4:47 PM detail and the exact excuse line make it feel real, not generic.
- Good instinct reframing "the data was never missing, it was just scattered" — takes blame off people, puts it on the system.
- The closing line lands well. It calls back to the "person on leave" problem from the opening. That's the kind of payoff that makes an explainer feel written, not assembled.
- One fix: the integrations line names four systems in one breath. Reads like a spec sheet. Either cut to two or three, or split across two sentences so it doesn't sound like a features list.

Review 2 — Direct-response copywriter
- One clear promise, held all the way through: ask instead of search. Don't dilute that with a second idea.
- No explicit call to action at the end. If this runs as a paid ad, you need an end card: visit site, book a demo, whatever the next step is. If it's meant purely for organic or sales-enablement use, the current close works fine as is.
- The security section reads a little like a disclaimer dropped in, not a benefit. Consider making "your data stays yours" feel like reassurance the viewer wanted to hear, not a caveat you had to add.
- "Gives you one thing back... An answer" repeats "answer" twice close together. Tighten one of those.

Review 3 — VO and production
- Timing checks out. About 180 words lands at 70-80 seconds at a normal ad-read pace. Inside your 60-90 second brief.
- "Buried in your ERP. Sitting in an inbox. Mentioned once on last week's sales call." is written as three short beats, which is good for VO. Keep that rhythm rather than merging them back into one long sentence.
- "What if you could just... ask?" — the ellipsis works as a pause cue, keep it.
- "Not a document. Not a dashboard. An answer." is the strongest line in the script for delivery. Punchy, easy for a voice actor to land with a beat between each.

Common thread across all three: tighten the integrations list and make the security line feel earned rather than bolted on.
