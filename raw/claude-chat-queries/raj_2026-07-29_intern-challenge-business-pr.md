Assistant: That's a fair addition, but let me flag something first: this isn't a clean match for either raj-eoxs-vault (no data pull needed) or sales-strategist (it's not a deal or GTM email) — it's really a standalone strategic/framing question about the intern program. I'll answer directly rather than force it into a skill.

On the business number: don't run this as a "founder vs 50 interns" contest. It sets up a comparison you're guaranteed to win in a way that either humiliates them on day one or, worse, teaches them that hitting your number is the bar — which is nonsense. You have 20 years and ~20,000 relationships. Freshers with zero industry credibility closing steel ERP or Podar deals in 6 months is not realistic, and if you frame it that way, the honest answer on day one is "probably close to zero," which kills morale before the program starts.

The metric that actually makes sense for this cohort is pipeline contribution, not closed revenue: qualified leads sourced, meetings booked, content/webinar reach, data cleanup that unblocks sales, demo requests generated. That's measurable, attributable per intern in the task manager, and realistic for MBA freshers. If you want a number to chase, chase "X qualified opportunities fed into the pipeline" or "Y webinar signups converted to demos" — not "$Z closed."

On PR: it can work, but only after the fact, not as the opening pitch. "We ran a 50-person, ₹10k/month fresher internship and it generated N qualified pipeline opportunities / helped close $X in expansion revenue" is a strong, verifiable recruiting and brand story — cheap talent cost, real business outcome, good employer-brand signal in a steel industry that runs on trust. But if you publicize a number before the 6 months are done, or inflate what interns actually sourced vs. what you or Ron closed yourself, you're setting up a credibility problem in a small, relationship-driven industry where people check claims against each other.

So: reframe day one from "beat my number" to "here's what good contribution looks like, and it's tracked," and hold the PR story until you have a real, defensible number to publish.
